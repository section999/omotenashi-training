title: ビザ (biza)
---

**Pronunciation:** [**bi**-za]
**Register:** Neutral/Formal, used in both everyday conversation and official contexts

ビザ is borrowed directly from the English word "visa," and it refers to the official endorsement in a passport that grants a foreign national permission to enter and remain in Japan for a specific period. Japan offers a range of visa categories: tourist, work, student, and several others, and the category a guest holds affects things like how long they can stay and whether an extension is possible. As a hospitality professional, you're not an immigration officer and you can't give legal advice, but having a working knowledge of the common visa types will help you handle questions at the front desk with confidence rather than a blank stare.

Worth noting for your day-to-day work: many countries have visa-exemption agreements with Japan, meaning their citizens can enter for up to 90 days without a ビザ at all. This covers large numbers of American, European, and Australian passport holders, so don't assume every international guest needed to apply for one. When a guest comes to you looking confused or worried about their visa status, the right move is to direct them politely to the Immigration Services Agency of Japan or to the nearest immigration office. A printed list of those contacts at the front desk is a small but thoughtful preparation.

## Example Sentences

**1.** 観光目的でご入国の場合、ビザが必要かどうかは国籍によって異なります。
*Kankou mokuteki de gonyuukoku no baai, biza ga hitsuyou ka dou ka wa kokuseki ni yotte kotonari masu.*
"Whether a visa is required for entry for tourism purposes depends on the guest's nationality."

**2.** お客様のビザの有効期限をフロントにてご確認いただけますでしょうか。
*Okyakusama no biza no yuukoukigen wo furonto nite gokakunin itadakemasu deshou ka.*
"Would you be able to confirm your visa's expiry date at the front desk?"

**3.** ビザの延長については、最寄りの入国管理局にお問い合わせください。
*Biza no enchoku ni tsuite wa, moyori no nyuukoku kanrikyoku ni otoiawase kudasai.*
"For visa extensions, please contact the nearest immigration office."

## Related Expressions

- **査証 (sashou)**: the formal, official Japanese word for visa; you'll find this in government documents and legal text, while ビザ is what everyone actually says in conversation.
- **在留資格 (zairyuu shikaku)**: the legal term for residence status or visa category; this is more specific than ビザ and refers to the exact classification under which a foreigner is permitted to stay in Japan.

## Common Mistakes

The most common mix-up is treating ビザ and パスポート (passport) as the same thing. The passport is the document itself, the physical booklet. The ビザ is the permission endorsed inside it or issued as a separate document. They work together, but they're not interchangeable. On pronunciation: ビザ is a clean two-mora word, *bi-za*. Students often stretch it into three morae as if trying to match the English, but that sounds unnatural in Japanese. Also, because visa exemptions are so common for tourists coming to Japan, avoid the assumption that every foreign guest has a visa stamp in their passport.

## Quiz

**Fill in the blank: ___**

**Question:** An official document that allows entry into a country is called ____ in Japanese.

**Answer:** ビザ

**Explanation:** ビザ (biza) means visa: the official document granting permission to enter a foreign country for a specific period.
