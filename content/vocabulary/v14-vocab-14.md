title: ガイド (gaido)
---

**Pronunciation:** [**gai**-do]
**Register:** Neutral, spoken hospitality, tourism services, written brochures

ガイド comes from the English "guide" and covers two related things: the person who leads tours, and the informational material (guidebook, audio device) that explains a place. In Japan's tourism industry, professional 観光ガイド are often formally licensed, particularly for national parks and major cultural sites, and guests who book a guided experience are trusting that person to manage timing, provide cultural context, and keep the group safe. The professional standard expected of a licensed ガイド is high, and recommending a good one reflects well on the hotel.

Hotel staff field questions about ガイド services regularly and need to be ready with clear options. Some properties arrange in-house guided experiences, while others keep a list of trusted agencies. Worth noting is the difference between a specialist ガイド and a general 案内 (annai), which means guidance or information more broadly: a front-desk assistant pointing a guest toward the elevator is doing 案内, while a licensed expert leading a temple walking tour is a ガイド. Getting that distinction right helps you match the right service to each guest's actual need.

## Example Sentences

**1.** 観光ガイドが名所を案内してくれました。
*Kankoukyaku gaido ga meisho wo annai shite kuremashita.*
"The tour guide showed us the famous places."

**2.** 当館では英語対応のガイドをご手配することができます。
*Toukan de wa eigo taiou no gaido wo otehaisuru koto ga dekimasu.*
"Our hotel can arrange an English-speaking guide for you."

**3.** 博物館では音声ガイドを借りることをお勧めします。
*Hakubutsukan de wa onsei gaido wo kariru koto wo osusume shimasu.*
"We recommend renting an audio guide at the museum."

## Related Expressions

- **案内人 (annai-nin)**: a general guide or usher; less specialized than ガイド, often used for in-house hotel escorts or someone directing guests to a venue
- **ツアーコンダクター (tsuaa kondakutaa)**: a tour conductor who manages group logistics; distinct from a ガイド, whose focus is on explanation and cultural interpretation rather than scheduling

## Common Mistakes

In spoken hospitality Japanese, ガイド primarily refers to a person or a specific printed or audio product, not just any source of information. Saying 「ガイドはどこですか」 can be interpreted as asking for a person or a physical guidebook depending on context, so adding 人 (hito) or 本 (hon) makes the meaning clear. Students also sometimes swap ガイドする with 案内する when giving directions, but 案内する is much more natural in hotel settings and is the phrase your guests will recognize and appreciate.

## Quiz

**Fill in the blank: ___**

**Question:** A person who provides information and directions to tourists is called ____ in Japanese.

**Answer:** ガイド

**Explanation:** ガイド (gaido) means guide: a person or tool that provides information, directions, and context for tourists at attractions.
