title: 角 (kado)
---

**Pronunciation:** **ka**-do
**Register:** Neutral — widely used in both casual and formal speech; in hotel contexts, paired with polite request forms for directions.

角 (kado) means "corner" and serves as one of the most practical landmark references in Japanese wayfinding communication. In hospitality settings, abstract directions ("turn right after a while") are far less useful than direction-plus-landmark combinations ("turn right at the next corner"), and 角 is the landmark of choice in corridors, streets, and building interiors. The word refers specifically to the point where a path changes direction — a street corner, a corridor junction, a room corner — giving guests a clear visual target to walk toward before acting on the instruction. This precision is a hallmark of thoughtful guest guidance.

In hotel communication, 角 most frequently appears as 次の角 (the next corner), 最初の角 (the first corner), or 二つ目の角 (the second corner), combined with the verb 曲がる: 次の角を右に曲がってください. It is also used in retail and restaurant contexts when directing guests around the exterior of a building or through a shopping complex. Staff should make sure to clarify which corner to turn at when multiple intersections are visible, as ambiguity at corners is a leading cause of guest misdirection.

## Example Sentences

**1.** 次の角を右に曲がってください。
*Tsugi no kado wo migi ni magatte kudasai.*
"Please turn right at the next corner."

**2.** 二つ目の角を左に曲がると、レストランのご入口がございます。
*Futatsu-me no kado wo hidari ni magaru to, resutoran no go-iriguchi ga gozaimasu.*
"Turn left at the second corner and you will find the restaurant entrance."

**3.** この角を曲がると正面に駐車場がございます。
*Kono kado wo magaru to shoumen ni chuushajou ga gozaimasu.*
"Turn at this corner and the parking lot will be directly ahead of you."

## Related Expressions

- **交差点 (kousaten)** — "intersection"; used for larger road crossings with traffic signals, while 角 typically refers to a turning point at a corner without implying a full road crossing
- **曲がり角 (magarikado)** — "turning corner"; a compound word combining 曲がる and 角, used in both literal directions and metaphorically (a turning point in life); in hospitality, 角 alone is sufficient and more natural

## Common Mistakes

Learners often drop 角 and simply say 右に曲がってください without specifying where, leaving guests to guess which turn to make — always name the specific corner. Another error is confusing 角 with 交差点 and using them interchangeably; 角 suggests a simpler corner or corridor junction, while 交差点 implies a full road intersection with traffic signals. Failing to count corners explicitly (次の, 二つ目の) when there are multiple turns ahead is a frequent cause of guests going too far or not far enough.

## Quiz

**スーパーはこの道の____にございます。
(The supermarket is at the corner of this street.)**

- A. 角 (corner)
- B. 奥 (back / deeper in)
- C. 入口 (entrance)
- D. 隣 (next to / adjacent)

**Correct Answer:** 角 (corner)

**Explanation:** 角 (corner) is a natural navigation landmark — saying 〜の角にございます (is at the corner of〜) gives guests a clear visual target to aim for rather than an abstract direction.
