**Pronunciation:** ai-shii-**kaa**-do
**Register:** Neutral — a standard term used across all hospitality contexts when advising guests on cashless payment for transport; widely understood by both Japanese and international travellers.

ICカード (IC kaado) stands for "Integrated Circuit card" and refers to the rechargeable contactless smart cards used throughout Japan for paying train and bus fares, as well as making purchases at convenience stores, vending machines, and some restaurants. The most famous examples are Suica (issued by JR East) and Pasmo (issued by Tokyo Metro and related operators), though many regional equivalents exist across Japan. These cards work on a tap-and-go system — the guest simply touches the card to the sensor at a ticket gate or bus reader — eliminating the need to calculate and purchase the correct 切符 for each journey. This makes 交通機関 travel significantly faster and less stressful for visitors.

Hotel staff who recommend ICカード to newly arrived guests provide genuine service value. Many international guests are unaware that these cards can be purchased and charged at vending machines in the arrivals hall of major airports. For hotels near busy tourist areas, suggesting the guest add enough balance (チャージ, chaaiji) for several days of travel is practical advice. Some ICカード — particularly Suica — can now be added to an Apple Wallet or Google Wallet, making physical card management unnecessary. A helpful staff phrase is 「ICカードをお持ちでしたら、いちいち切符をお買い求めいただかなくても便利にご移動いただけます」("If you have an IC card, you can travel conveniently without purchasing individual tickets each time").

## Example Sentences

**1.** ICカードで改札を通りました。
*IC kaado de kaisatsu wo toorimashita.*
"I passed through the ticket gate with an IC card."

**2.** SuicaやPasmoなどのICカードは、電車だけでなくコンビニでもご利用いただけます。
*Suika ya Pasumo nado no IC kaado wa, densha dake de naku konbini de mo goriyou itadakemasu.*
"IC cards such as Suica and Pasmo can be used not only on trains but also at convenience stores."

**3.** ICカードへのチャージは駅の自動券売機でいつでも行っていただけます。
*IC kaado e no chaaiji wa eki no jidou kenbaiki de itsudemo okonatte itadakemasu.*
"You can top up your IC card at station ticket vending machines at any time."

## Related Expressions

- **Suica (スイカ)** — the most widely used ICカード in eastern Japan, issued by JR East; the name plays on 水か (like water) and sui ca (from "swipe card"), and its penguin mascot is iconic
- **チャージ (chaaiji)** — to top up or reload money onto an ICカード; from the English "charge," this is the standard verb used for adding balance at vending machines or convenience stores

## Common Mistakes

Learners sometimes assume an ICカード purchased in one region works everywhere in Japan — while most major cards have nationwide compatibility now, some older or region-specific cards have limitations, and it is worth confirming coverage. A grammatical error is saying ICカードをチャージする — while this is understood, the more natural phrasing is ICカードにチャージする (particle に). Also, do not confuse ICカード (transport/payment card) with クレジットカード (credit card) — guests asking about paying by card at the hotel may mean either, and staff should clarify which type of card is being discussed.

## Quiz

**Fill in the blank: ___**

**Question:** A rechargeable smart card for transportation payments is called ____ in Japanese.

**Answer:** ICカード

**Explanation:** ICカード (IC kaado) means IC card — a rechargeable contactless card used on trains and buses, such as Suica or Pasmo.
