title: ご案内いたします (go-annai itashimasu)
---

**ご案内いたします** means "I will guide you" or "I will show you." *Annai* (案内) means guidance, escort, or information, elevated by the honorific *go-* prefix and completed with *itashimasu*, the humble form of "to do." The phrase is a declaration of service: *I am taking responsibility for getting you where you need to go.*

In hospitality, guiding a guest is one of the most intimate forms of service. Unlike answering a question at a desk, guiding places the staff member physically alongside the guest — creating a small, temporary bond of movement and trust. *Go-annai itashimasu* is the formal declaration that initiates this bond. It is said when picking up a guest from the lobby, when escorting them to their room, when leading them to a facility, or when walking them to the entrance at departure.

The phrase carries responsibility: once you say *go-annai itashimasu*, you are committed to the guiding role until the guest arrives at their destination. This means walking at their pace, announcing hazards, opening doors, and giving them your full attention for the duration of the walk. The quality of the guide walk is itself a form of hospitality.

## Example Sentences

**1.** お部屋までご案内いたします。こちらへどうぞ。
*O-heya made go-annai itashimasu. Kochira e dōzo.*
"I will show you to your room. Please come this way."

**2.** レストランまでご案内いたします。足元にお気をつけください。
*Resutoran made go-annai itashimasu. Ashimoto ni o-ki wo tsukete kudasai.*
"I will guide you to the restaurant. Please watch your step."

**3.** チェックアウトの際には、出口までご案内いたします。
*Chekku-auto no sai ni wa, deguchi made go-annai itashimasu.*
"At checkout, I will escort you to the entrance."

## Related Expressions

- **こちらへどうぞ (kochira e dōzo)** — "Please come this way" — the movement invitation that follows *go-annai itashimasu*
- **お連れいたします (o-tsure itashimasu)** — "I will accompany you" — slightly warmer/more personal, sometimes used in VIP contexts
- **ご案内申し上げます (go-annai mōshiagemasu)** — Even more formal version of *go-annai itashimasu*; used in highly elevated service contexts or formal announcements

## Common Mistakes

A common mistake is saying *go-annai itashimasu* and then not fully committing to the escort — walking too fast, not opening doors, not announcing hazards, or leaving the guest to find the final destination on their own. The phrase creates an obligation that must be honoured. A second mistake is using *go-annai itashimasu* only for room escorts — any time a guest needs to be physically guided anywhere (restaurant, spa, meeting room, elevator), the phrase and the attentive escort are appropriate.

## Quiz

**A guest has just checked in and is ready to go to their room. You have their key card. What is the ideal phrase to begin the escort?**

- A. エレベーターはあちらです。(The elevator is that way — delegates navigation rather than guiding)
- B. ついてきてください。(Please follow me — too casual and direct)
- C. お部屋までご案内いたします。こちらへどうぞ。(I will show you to your room. Please come this way.)
- D. 自分でどうぞ。(Please go on your own — entirely inappropriate)

**Correct Answer:** お部屋までご案内いたします。こちらへどうぞ。(I will show you to your room. Please come this way.)

**Explanation:** *Go-annai itashimasu* declares the commitment to guide; *kochira e dōzo* with an open-palm gesture initiates the movement. Together they create a seamless, warm beginning to the escort. The guest should never have to navigate independently when a staff member is available — guiding is a service, not an option.
