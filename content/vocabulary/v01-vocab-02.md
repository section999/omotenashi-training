**おはようございます** is the standard morning greeting in Japanese, used from waking until roughly 10:00 or 11:00 AM depending on the setting. In hospitality, it is often the very first phrase a guest hears on any given day. Whether delivered at the breakfast buffet, in the corridor, or at the front desk, the quality of that moment shapes their entire morning. A warm, clear おはようございます tells the guest that staff are present and ready.

The phrase has a historical warmth built into it. It derives from *hayai* (早い), meaning "early." The original sense was something like "one is out early, and I respect that", an acknowledgement of the other person's presence and effort. Holding that sense while delivering the greeting each morning tends to make it feel more alive rather than routine.

In hotel settings, close attention to the time boundary is important. Using a morning greeting in the afternoon, even by accident, signals inattentiveness to the guest. Greeting a guest who has just come down to breakfast at 9 AM with こんにちは feels off. Staff should read the guest's state and the clock, and use the greeting that fits the actual moment

## Related Expressions

| Expression | Description |
|---|---|
| こんにちは<br>konnichiwa | Daytime greeting (approximately 11 AM to 6 PM). The natural transition from おはようございます as the morning moves forward. |
| おやすみなさい<br>oyasumi nasai | Good night. The complementary farewell for when guests are heading to bed in the evening. |

## Common Mistakes

Using the casual おはよう (without ございます) in a professional setting is the most common slip. It sounds overly familiar with guests and with colleagues staff do not know well, and the difference in register is noticeable even to guests who speak very little Japanese. The casual form tends to persist because it is often the version learned first; drilling the full form until it comes out naturally helps remove that habit. Using おはようございます past late morning should also be avoided. If one is uncertain whether it is still morning for a particular guest, say a late sleeper coming down just before noon, defaulting to こんにちは is safer than risking the mismatch.

## Quiz

**A guest arrives at the breakfast buffet at 8:30 AM. What is the appropriate greeting?**

- A. こんにちは。(Good afternoon.)
- B. おはようございます。(Good morning.)
- C. おはよう。(Morning: casual)
- D. こんばんは。(Good evening.)

**Correct Answer:** おはようございます。(Good morning.)

**Explanation:** おはようございます, the full polite form, is the only appropriate morning greeting in professional hospitality. The casual おはよう drops the register too far for a guest interaction, while こんにちは and こんばんは belong to the wrong time of day entirely.
