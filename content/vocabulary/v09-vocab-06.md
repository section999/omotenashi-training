title: 滞在 (taizai)
---
**Pronunciation:** [tai-**zai**]
**Register:** Formal -- used across all guest-facing communication and internal records

滞在 refers to the full period a guest spends at the property. Where 到着日 and 出発日 mark the endpoints, 滞在 captures the experience in between as a continuous whole. In omotenashi philosophy, thinking in terms of 滞在 rather than isolated moments encourages staff to consider the guest's comfort, mood, and needs across the entire visit, not just during check-in or checkout. The phrase お滞在中はごゆっくりおくつろぎください (please relax during one's stay) is a standard expression in Japanese hospitality, and the reason it works is that it frames the hotel as a sanctuary for the whole of the guest's time there, not just the first few minutes. That holistic perspective is a recognizable quality of the Japanese hospitality mindset.

A common error is to encounter 滞在 in billing summaries (ご滞在中の料金), service announcements (滞在中はルームサービスをご利用いただけます), and post-stay follow-ups (ご滞在はいかがでしたでしょうか). It also comes up naturally in conversations when staff check in on a guest's overall comfort. Choosing 滞在 over a narrower term signals that one is attending to the whole experience rather than a single transaction. Note that when calculating charges or planning services, 滞在 encompasses everything from the moment of arrival to the moment of departure, making it the most comprehensive framing available\n## Example Sentences

**1.** お滞在中はごゆっくりおくつろぎください。
*O-taizai chuu wa go-yukkuri o-kutsurogi kudasai.*
"Please relax and enjoy the stay with us."

**2.** ご滞在は3泊4日のご予定でございますね。
*Go-taizai wa san-paku yokka no go-yotei de gozaimasu ne.*
"Your stay is scheduled for 3 nights and 4 days."

**3.** ご滞在中にご不便がございましたら、いつでもフロントまでお申しつけください。
*Go-taizai chuu ni go-fuben ga gozaimashitara, itsudemo furonto made o-moushitsuke kudasai.*
"If you experience any inconvenience during the stay, please let us know at the front desk at any time."


## Related Expressions

- **宿泊 (shukuhaku)**: focused specifically on overnight accommodation; 滞在 is broader and can include day-use visits, while 宿泊 always implies spending at least one night
- **お過ごし (o-sugoshi)**: a warmer, softer expression for how the guest is spending their time; used in phrases like お過ごしはいかがでしょうか as a gentle check on the guest's comfort during their stay


## Common Mistakes

A common error is to substitute 宿泊 for 滞在 in the phrase お滞在中, producing お宿泊中, which sounds unnatural in spoken hospitality Japanese. 滞在中 is the established, standard construction for "during one's stay" and should not be swapped out. However, another mistake worth addressing is treating 滞在 as synonymous with 泊, the counter for nights. A guest who says they have a 3日間の滞在 is describing a three-day stay, which may include a checkout day with no overnight charge. 3泊, on the other hand, means exactly three billable nights. Conflating the two leads to billing confusion that takes more time to fix than it takes to prevent.

## Quiz

**Which term describes the overall time a guest spends in the hotel?**

- A. 滞在 (stay)
- B. 宿泊日 (stay date)
- C. 到着日 (arrival date)
- D. 出発日 (departure date)

**Correct Answer:** 滞在 (stay)

**Explanation:** 滞在 describes the overall time a guest spends at the hotel as a continuous experience, from the moment they arrive to the moment they leave. 宿泊日 names the calendar dates, 到着日 marks only the first day, and 出発日 marks only the last, none of these capture the lived experience of the stay as a whole the way 滞在 does.
