title: ナイフ (naifu)
---

**Pronunciation:** **na**-i-fu
**Register:** Neutral — as a loanword (gairaigo) from English, ナイフ is understood universally and carries no particular formality level on its own. In guest-facing contexts, it is used within polite sentence structures using humble or respectful verb forms; among staff, it appears in plain speech during setup and service briefings.

ナイフ is the Japanese word for a knife, borrowed directly from English, and in a hotel or restaurant context it refers specifically to the table knife used as part of Western-style (洋食) cutlery. Proper table setting in Western dining is a visible signal of service quality, and the correct placement of ナイフ — to the right of the plate, blade facing inward — is a standard skill for all F&B service staff. Guests dining in a hotel restaurant expect their cutlery to be in place before food is served, and any missing or incorrectly placed item reflects on the overall service standard.

In daily restaurant and room service operations, staff refer to ナイフ when setting tables, responding to guest requests for additional cutlery, or describing the utensils that accompany a specific dish. A common phrase is ナイフをご用意いたします ("we will prepare a knife"), used when a guest requests one or when staff announce they are setting the table for a meal. Staff should also know how to pair ナイフ with フォーク (fork) in standard table setting language, and should be able to offer or clarify cutlery choices for guests with special needs.

## Example Sentences

**1.** お料理に合わせてナイフとフォークをご用意いたします。
*O-ryouri ni awasete naifu to fooku wo go-youi itashimasu.*
"We will prepare a knife and fork to match your dish."

**2.** ナイフが必要でございましたら、すぐにお持ちいたします。
*Naifu ga hitsuyou de gozaimashita ra, sugu ni o-mochi itashimasu.*
"If you need a knife, I will bring one right away."

**3.** ステーキナイフをご用意してもよろしいでしょうか。
*Suteeki naifu wo go-youi shite mo yoroshii deshou ka.*
"May I prepare a steak knife for you?"

## Related Expressions

- **フォーク (fooku)** — Fork. ナイフ and フォーク are the two primary Western cutlery items and are almost always mentioned together in table setting and service contexts; knowing both allows staff to address full cutlery needs in a single statement.
- **箸 (hashi)** — Chopsticks. When a guest prefers Japanese-style utensils or when the dish is Japanese cuisine, 箸 may replace or accompany Western cutlery; being able to offer both ナイフ・フォーク and 箸 shows attentiveness to guest preference.

## Common Mistakes

Learners sometimes pronounce ナイフ with three clearly separate syllables (na-i-fu) in a way that sounds hesitant — in natural speech it flows smoothly as a two-beat word with the stress on the first mora. A common service error is removing the ナイフ during a course change without replacing it when needed for the next course — always assess whether the incoming dish requires a knife before clearing. Finally, staff should not assume all guests need a ナイフ; for dishes like pasta or salad, offering フォークとスプーン (fork and spoon) and checking whether a knife is also needed shows attentiveness rather than automatically placing all cutlery.

## Quiz

**お料理に合わせて____をご用意いたします。
(We will prepare a knife to match your dish.)**

- A. ナイフ (knife)
- B. フォーク (fork)
- C. スプーン (spoon)
- D. 箸 (chopsticks)

**Correct Answer:** ナイフ (knife)

**Explanation:** ナイフをご用意いたします — ご用意いたします (we will prepare) is the humble form for setting or providing items, used widely in F&B table service.
