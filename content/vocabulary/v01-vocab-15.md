**お手伝いしましょうか** means "may I help you?" or "shall I assist you?" It is the proactive, attentive staff member's most useful phrase, offered not in response to a complaint, but in anticipation of a need. The word *tetsudai* (手伝い) means assistance or help, elevated by the honorific *o-* prefix. The しましょうか ending is a polite offer/proposal form: "shall I...?"

The key distinction between this phrase and the more passive "is there anything I can help you with?" (何かお手伝いできることはございますか) is timing. お手伝いしましょうか is said proactively, in the moment, when a staff member sees a guest who looks like they might need assistance: struggling with luggage, looking around as if searching for something, standing at a confusing junction. It is the phrase of noticed need.

In the philosophy of omotenashi, waiting for a guest to ask for help is already too late. A hospitality professional anticipates the need, moves toward the guest, and offers お手伝いしましょうか before the guest has to formulate a request. This proactive care, 気が付く (気が付く), or "noticing," is a defining quality of excellent service. A common error is knowing the phrase but hesitating to use it proactively. This hesitation should be addressed through practice.

## Related Expressions

| Expression | Description |
|---|---|
| 何かお手伝いできることはございますか<br>nanika o-tetsudai dekiru koto wa gozaimasu ka | "Is there anything I can help you with?": a more open-ended version, inviting the guest to name a need |
| ご案内いたします<br>go-annai itashimasu | "I will guide/show you": follows naturally after the guest accepts help |
| いかがなさいましたか<br>ikaga nasaimashita ka | "What seems to be the matter?": used when a guest appears distressed or confused |

## Common Mistakes

The most common mistake is waiting for a guest to make eye contact or directly ask before offering help. In omotenashi-oriented service, the initiative comes from the staff member, not the guest. A second mistake is offering お手伝いしましょうか in a tone that feels routine or performative. The phrase works best when it sounds noticed and offered, not scripted.

## Quiz

**You notice a guest standing in the lobby with heavy bags, looking around as if searching for directions. What do you do?**

- A. Wait at the front desk in case they approach.
- B. Make eye contact and nod: if they come over, help them.
- C. Walk toward them, make eye contact, and say: お荷物をお持ちでしょうか。お手伝いしましょうか。(Do you have luggage? May I help you?)
- D. Call out "Can I help?" from across the lobby.

**Correct Answer:** Walk toward them, make eye contact, and say: お荷物をお持ちでしょうか。お手伝いしましょうか。(Do you have luggage? May I help you?)

**Explanation:** Omotenashi means noticing a need before it becomes a request, which is why walking toward the guest and offering help proactively is the complete response here. Waiting at the desk, making a passive nod, or calling across the lobby all shift the burden back to the guest, which is the opposite of what good service looks like.
