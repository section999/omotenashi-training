title: 満室 (manshitsu)
---

満室 means "fully booked" or "no vacancies," indicating that every room in the property is currently occupied. It is the direct opposite of 空室 (vacant room), and communicating 満室 to a guest who has arrived without a reservation — or one whose modification request cannot be accommodated — is one of the most delicate situations a front desk professional faces. In Japanese hospitality culture, delivering negative information requires even greater care than delivering positive news; the manner in which 満室 is communicated can preserve or destroy a guest's goodwill toward the property.

The standard professional approach is to prefix the announcement with an apology (大変申し訳ございませんが), specify the time frame clearly (本日は, meaning "for today"), and if possible, offer an alternative such as a referral to a nearby partner hotel. The full phrase 本日は満室でございます (we are fully booked today) uses ございます for formality and 本日 to limit the scope to the current day, implying that availability may change. Offering a waitlist option or suggesting a future date demonstrates that the hotel values the guest even when it cannot serve them immediately.

## Example Sentences

**1.** 大変申し訳ございませんが、本日は満室でございます。
*Taihen mōshiwake gozaimasen ga, honjitsu wa manshitsu de gozaimasu.*
"We sincerely apologise, but we are fully booked for today."

**2.** 本日は満室でございますが、明日以降でしたらご空室をご用意できます。
*Honjitsu wa manshitsu de gozaimasu ga, ashita ikō deshitara go-kūshitsu o go-yōi dekimasu.*
"We are fully booked today, but we can accommodate you from tomorrow onward."

**3.** 満室の際は、近隣のホテルをご案内することも可能でございます。
*Manshitsu no sai wa, kinrin no hoteru o go-annai suru koto mo kanō de gozaimasu.*
"When we are fully booked, we can also direct you to nearby hotels."

## Related Expressions

- **空室 (kūshitsu)** — the opposite term meaning "vacant room"; always check for 空室 before declaring 満室, as last-minute cancellations can create unexpected availability.
- **キャンセル待ち (kyanseru machi)** — a waitlist for cancellations; offering this to a guest when 満室 is the situation shows proactive hospitality and helps recover the potential booking.

## Common Mistakes

The most serious mistake is stating 満室です without the accompanying apology — in Japanese hospitality, bad news must always be softened with 申し訳ございません or 大変恐れ入りますが. Learners also sometimes declare 満室 without first checking current availability via the reservation system, which can result in turning away a guest when a room is actually available. Additionally, using the casual form 満室だ or 満室ね in any professional context is entirely inappropriate and would be considered rude.

## Quiz

**Which expression is most appropriate when the hotel is fully booked?**

- A. 満室です (fully booked)
- B. 本日は満室でございます (we are fully booked today)
- C. 満室ある (there are vacancies)
- D. 満室ね (fully booked (casual))

**Correct Answer:** 本日は満室でございます (we are fully booked today)

**Explanation:** 本日は満室でございます uses ございます and specifies 本日 (today), making the refusal polite and informative.
