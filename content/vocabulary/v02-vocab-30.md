**Pronunciation:** **fu**-ron-to-**ka**-ka-ri
**Register:** Formal — フロント係 is a professional designation used within the hotel industry. Staff use it in self-introductions, internal communications, and when guests ask who to contact for front desk services. It carries a sense of designated responsibility, and when combined with formal copulas (でございます), it produces a polished and professional self-identification appropriate for any Japanese hotel environment.

フロント係 (furonto kakari) means "front desk staff" or "front desk attendant" — the person assigned to manage the hotel's front desk operations. The term 係 (kakari) specifically denotes a person assigned to or responsible for a particular role or duty, giving フロント係 a precise, role-based meaning that goes beyond simply "someone who works at the front." These staff members are the face of the hotel: they are the first people guests encounter upon arrival and the last they interact with upon departure. Their professionalism, language ability, and interpersonal warmth shape the guest's entire impression of the property.

フロント係 are responsible for a wide range of duties: check-in and check-out, reservation management, payment processing, key handling, guest inquiries, and coordinating with other departments on behalf of guests. In Japanese hospitality culture, the フロント係 must maintain keigo (respectful language) at all times and embody the values of omotenashi — anticipating needs, responding gracefully to requests and complaints, and ensuring every guest feels genuinely welcomed. The self-introduction 私はフロント係でございます is a foundational phrase, but equally important is the ability to say 何かございましたら、いつでもおっしゃってください (please feel free to ask if you need anything at any time).

## Example Sentences

**1.** 私はフロント係でございます。何かお手伝いできることがあればおっしゃってください。
*Watashi wa furonto kakari de gozaimasu. Nanika o-tetsudai dekiru koto ga areba osshatte kudasai.*
"I am the front desk staff. Please let me know if there is anything I can help you with."

**2.** フロント係は二十四時間対応しております。
*Furonto kakari wa nijū-yo-jikan taiō shite orimasu.*
"Our front desk staff are available twenty-four hours a day."

**3.** ご不明な点はフロント係までお気軽にお申し付けください。
*Go-fumei na ten wa furonto kakari made o-kigaru ni o-mōshitsuke kudasai.*
"Please feel free to contact the front desk staff with any questions."

## Related Expressions

- **フロントスタッフ (furonto sutaffu)** — front desk staff (collective), a more casual loanword variant sometimes used in internal communications or staff meetings. While フロント係 emphasizes the assigned role and individual responsibility (係), フロントスタッフ is a broader collective reference to the team.
- **コンシェルジュ (konshieruju)** — concierge, a specialized guest services role that overlaps with but is distinct from フロント係. While フロント係 handles administrative check-in/check-out procedures, the コンシェルジュ focuses on personalized guest requests, recommendations, and arrangements. In smaller hotels, フロント係 may perform both functions.

## Common Mistakes

A common mistake is introducing oneself simply as フロントです, which is too abbreviated and imprecise — the full form 私はフロント係でございます clearly identifies the role and uses appropriately formal language. Learners sometimes translate 係 literally as "manager" or "supervisor" when explaining the role in English, but 係 simply means "the person assigned to" a task — it implies responsibility, not seniority. Staff should also remember that being フロント係 means being proactive: waiting passively for guests to ask questions contradicts the omotenashi spirit that the role represents.

## Quiz

**Which is the most appropriate self-introduction as front desk staff?**

- A. フロント係です (I am front desk staff)
- B. 私はフロント係でございます (I am the front desk staff)
- C. フロント係する (work at front desk)
- D. フロント係ね (front desk staff (casual))

**Correct Answer:** 私はフロント係でございます (I am the front desk staff)

**Explanation:** 私はフロント係でございます uses the formal 私 and ございます — professional and polite for any self-introduction in a hospitality context.
