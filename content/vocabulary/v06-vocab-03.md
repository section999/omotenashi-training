---

**Pronunciation:** ku-**jou**
**Register:** Formal — primarily used in management and internal contexts; when speaking directly to a guest, staff often choose softer language unless reflecting the guest's own words.

苦情 (kujou) means a complaint or grievance, and carries a stronger emotional weight than 問題 or トラブル. It implies that the guest has experienced genuine dissatisfaction and feels strongly enough to voice it. In Japanese hospitality culture, receiving a 苦情 is treated not as an attack but as an important signal — an opportunity to correct a flaw in service that might otherwise go unnoticed. The omotenashi philosophy holds that a guest who complains is still giving the establishment a chance to improve, and that chance must be honoured with sincerity and humility.

When a 苦情 is received, the professionally correct sequence is to listen without interrupting, to acknowledge the guest's feelings with a genuine apology, and only then to explain what action will be taken. Staff should never minimise the complaint or offer excuses before expressing empathy. In larger hotels, 苦情 are documented and reviewed regularly to identify patterns, and front-line staff are trained to escalate appropriately when the nature of the complaint exceeds their authority to resolve.

## Example Sentences

**1.** お客様の苦情を真摯に受け止め、早急に対応いたします。
*O-kyaku-sama no kujou wo shinshi ni uketome, sakkyu ni taiou itashimasu.*
"We will take your complaint sincerely to heart and respond as quickly as possible."

**2.** ご苦情をいただきまして、ありがとうございます。改善に努めてまいります。
*Go-kujou wo itadakimashite, arigatou gozaimasu. Kaizen ni tsutomete mairimasu.*
"Thank you for bringing your complaint to our attention. We will strive to make improvements."

**3.** 苦情内容を詳しくお聞かせいただけますでしょうか。
*Kujou naiyou wo kuwashiku o-kikase itadakemasu deshoo ka.*
"Could you please tell me the details of your complaint?"

## Related Expressions

- **クレーム (kuremu)** — often implies a stronger, more formal or confrontational complaint that may require management involvement or compensation; 苦情 can be softer in some contexts.
- **ご意見 (go-iken)** — means "your opinion/feedback"; using this term instead of 苦情 softens the interaction and can make the guest feel their input is valued rather than categorised as a problem.

## Common Mistakes

A common error is for staff to apologise reflexively before fully understanding the 苦情, which can imply responsibility for something that was not the hotel's fault and create legal complications. The correct approach is to first express empathy (ご不便をおかけして申し訳ございません) and then gather the full picture. Learners also sometimes confuse 苦情 with 文句 (monku), which is more informal and can sound dismissive if used in professional speech — 苦情 should always be used in formal service contexts.

## Quiz

**Which phrase correctly shows you are taking a complaint seriously?**

- A. 苦情を真摯に受け止めます (we will take your complaint seriously)
- B. 苦情無視 (ignoring complaints)
- C. 苦情ダメ (complaints not accepted)
- D. 苦情知らない (don't know about the complaint)

**Correct Answer:** 苦情を真摯に受け止めます (we will take your complaint seriously)

**Explanation:** 苦情を真摯に受け止めます — 真摯に (sincerely/earnestly) and 受け止めます (to accept/receive) together express genuine accountability rather than defensive deflection.
