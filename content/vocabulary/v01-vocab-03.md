title: こんにちは (konnichiwa)
---

**こんにちは** is the standard daytime greeting in Japanese, used from approximately late morning (around 11:00 AM) through early evening (around 6:00 PM). Its origin is slightly archaic, it was once part of a longer phrase that meant something like "How is today treating you?" Over time it shortened, but that underlying sense of noticing the other person stayed. In a hospitality setting, that is what gives it weight: one is not just acknowledging the guest's presence, one is recognising the day they are having.

こんにちは most often appears mid-stay. A guest passes through the lobby in the afternoon, returns from sightseeing, or approaches the concierge desk during daytime hours. It reads as warmer and more personal than the formal いらっしゃいませ, and in some moments it feels less transactional. Knowing when to use いらっしゃいませ (first contact, formal arrival) versus こんにちは (ongoing daytime encounters) is part of reading the situation correctly. Both are appropriate; the choice depends on context.

The time window for こんにちは matters. Using the phrase in the early morning or after dark shows a lack of attentiveness that experienced guests notice. It is a small slip, but in a service environment, small slips accumulate

## Related Expressions

| Expression | Description |
|---|---|
| おはようございます<br>ohayō gozaimasu | Morning greeting (until approximately 11 AM). Use this before こんにちは becomes appropriate for the time of day. |
| こんばんは<br>konbanwa | Evening greeting (from approximately 6 PM onward). The natural handoff from こんにちは as the day ends. |
| いらっしゃいませ<br>irasshaimase | Formal arrival welcome. Can precede こんにちは or stand alone on first contact with a guest. |

## Common Mistakes

The biggest mistake is getting the time window wrong: using こんにちは in the morning or after dark. A second common mistake is equally avoidable: mispronouncing the particle は as *ha*. In this phrase it is always read as *wa*, and getting it wrong draws attention in a way that undermines the greeting. The casual abbreviation *konchi* (コンチ) should be avoided in any professional setting, regardless of how friendly or relaxed the guest seems. The register one maintains with guests should stay consistent.

## Quiz

**A guest returns to the hotel lobby at 2:30 PM after a day of sightseeing. You see them near the elevator. What is the appropriate greeting?**

- A. おはようございます。(Good morning.)
- B. こんばんは。(Good evening.)
- C. こんにちは。(Good afternoon.)
- D. お疲れ様です。(Thank you for your hard work.)

**Correct Answer:** こんにちは。(Good afternoon.)

**Explanation:** At 2:30 PM, こんにちは is the correct greeting because it falls squarely in the daytime window. おはようございます and こんばんは belong to different parts of the day, and お疲れ様です is a collegial phrase reserved for staff, not for welcoming guests.
