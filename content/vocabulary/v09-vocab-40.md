title: 手配 (tehai)
---

**Pronunciation:** [te-**hai**]
**Register:** Formal -- used across hotel departments for any coordinated service

手配 means to arrange, organize, or coordinate something on a guest's behalf. The scope is wide: booking an airport transfer, reserving a restaurant table, arranging flowers for an anniversary, setting up a meeting room, or organizing a private car. What the word conveys is active ownership -- staff are not simply passing a request along, they are taking personal responsibility to see it through. That sense of quiet effort behind the scenes is very much at the heart of Japanese hospitality culture, where seamless service is valued precisely because the work that makes it seamless stays invisible.

When using 手配 in speech, 手配いたします is the expected humble form at luxury and upscale properties, while 手配します works in more casual settings. Before you say either one, confirm the key details first: departure time, number of people, destination, and any special requirements. Students often commit to 手配いたします too quickly, before they have all the information they need, and the result is an arrangement that misses the mark. Once you've confirmed the details and made the arrangement, follow up with the guest -- タクシーの手配が完了いたしました closes the loop and lets the guest know everything is taken care of.

## Example Sentences

**1.** 空港までのタクシーを手配いたします。
*Kuukou made no takushii wo tehai itashimasu.*
"We will arrange a taxi to the airport for you."

**2.** お部屋にお花を手配させていただきました。
*Oheya ni ohana wo tehai sasete itadakimashita.*
"We have arranged flowers for your room."

**3.** ご要望のレストランのご予約も手配いたします。
*Go-youbou no resutoran no go-yoyaku mo tehai itashimasu.*
"We will also arrange a reservation at your requested restaurant."

## Related Expressions

- **準備 (junbi)**: preparation; the focus here is on getting things physically ready yourself, rather than organizing or commissioning a service. 手配 and 準備 often go hand in hand when a request involves both coordination and setup.
- **調整 (chousei)**: adjustment or coordination; used when you're fine-tuning schedules or balancing competing requirements between multiple parties, rather than initiating the arrangement itself.

## Common Mistakes

A common error is saying 手配します before confirming what the guest actually needs -- this leads to incorrect arrangements that disappoint rather than delight. There is also frequent confusion between 手配 and 準備: 手配 is about organizing or commissioning a service, often involving a third party, while 準備 is about preparing something yourself. They sound similar in concept but point to different actions. One more thing to remember: after you have completed the 手配, always follow up with the guest. Leaving them to wonder whether their request was handled undermines all the care you put into making the arrangement in the first place.

## Quiz

**Guest asks: "Can you organize a taxi for me?" Which term applies?**

- A. 手配 (arrangement)
- B. 予約変更 (reservation change)
- C. 追加料金 (additional charge)
- D. 割引 (discount)

**Correct Answer:** 手配 (arrangement)

**Explanation:** 手配 is the term for actively arranging a service on a guest's behalf, and 手配いたします signals personal ownership of that coordination from start to finish, not just passing a message along. The other options all point in different directions: 予約変更 modifies a booking, 追加料金 is a charge, and 割引 is a price reduction, none of which describes the act of organizing something for a guest.
