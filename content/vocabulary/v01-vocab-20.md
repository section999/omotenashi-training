title: またお越しくださいませ (mata okoshi kudasaimase)
---

**またお越しくださいませ** means "please come again," but it carries considerably more warmth than its translation suggests. *Mata* (また) means "again," お越し (お越し) is the honorific for "coming/visiting," and くださいませ is the most polite form of "please do." The phrase is the hospitality professional's final act of care: a sincere, dignified invitation for the guest to return.

The timing and delivery of this phrase matter a great deal. It is the last impression the guest carries out the door, which means it has significant weight in shaping their memory of the entire stay. A deeply felt またお越しくださいませ, delivered with genuine eye contact, a bow, and the staff member's full attention, says: *you mattered to us. Come back when staff can.*

This phrase is often part of a farewell sequence: *honjitsu wa go-riyō arigatō gozaimashita* (thank one for their visit today), followed by お気をつけて (please take care), and closing with またお越しくださいませ (please come again). Each phrase in the sequence builds on the last, creating a complete and warm send-off


## Example Sentences

**ご利用ありがとうございました。またお越しくださいませ。**  
*Go-riyō arigatō gozaimashita. Mata o-koshi kudasaimase.*  
Thank you for staying with us. Please come again.

**本日はご来館いただきありがとうございました。またお越しくださいませ。**  
*Honjitsu wa go-raikan itadaki arigatō gozaimashita. Mata o-koshi kudasaimase.*  
Thank you for visiting us today. Please come again.

**またのお越しを、スタッフ一同心よりお待ち申し上げております。**  
*Mata no o-koshi wo, sutaffu ichidō kokoro yori o-machi mōshiagemasu.*  
All of our staff look forward with all their hearts to your next visit.

## Related Expressions

| Expression | Description |
|---|---|
| お気をつけてお帰りください<br>o-ki wo tsukete o-kaeri kudasai | "Please take care going home": the safety farewell that precedes またお越しくださいませ |
| またのお越しをお待ちしております<br>mata no o-koshi wo o-machi shite orimasu | "We look forward to their next visit": slightly more formal and often used in written farewells |
| いつでもまたお越しください<br>itsudemo mata o-koshi kudasai | "Please come again any time": adds warmth and openness to the invitation |

## Common Mistakes

The most common mistake is delivering this phrase while already turned away or distracted. It should be the last thing staff give the guest their full attention for. A second mistake is omitting it entirely in busy periods; the farewell is expected. Every guest deserves to leave with this phrase. A third mistake is saying it too quickly, letting it sound like a procedural checkbox rather than a genuine invitation.

## Quiz

**A guest is departing after a two-night stay. You have thanked them and wished them a safe journey. What is the ideal final phrase?**

- A. さようなら。(Goodbye: too final and informal)
- B. またお越しくださいませ。(Please come again.)
- C. ありがとうございます。(Thank you: present tense, already said)
- D. いってらっしゃいませ。(Please go and come back: for temporary departures only)

**Correct Answer:** またお越しくださいませ。(Please come again.)

**Explanation:** またお越しくださいませ is the ideal closing phrase because it leaves the door warmly open for a future visit, making it the natural conclusion after thanks and a safety farewell. さようなら carries a tone of finality that feels cold for hotel hospitality, and いってらっしゃいませ is only right when the guest will return the same day.
