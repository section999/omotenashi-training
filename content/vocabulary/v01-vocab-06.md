title: 申し訳ございません (moushiwake gozaimasen)
---

**申し訳ございません** is the apex of Japanese apology language. Broken down: *mōshi* (to say, to speak), *wake* (reason, justification), literally, "there is no reason I can give for this." This etymology is not only poetic; it also shapes the philosophy behind the phrase. When a staff member says *moushiwake gozaimasen*, one is not offering an explanation or deflecting blame. They are saying: *I have no excuse. What happened was unacceptable. I accept responsibility.*

In Japanese hospitality, the manner of the apology matters as much as the words. *Moushiwake gozaimasen* is delivered with a sincere, deep bow (typically 45°), eye contact before and after, and a calm, unhurried tone. The physical expression of the apology is inseparable from its meaning. Rushing through the phrase while multitasking communicates the opposite of what the words say.

*Moushiwake gozaimasen* is different from lighter expressions. It is used when something has gone wrong, a room not ready, a reservation lost, a meal incorrect, an unreasonable wait. For minor social intrusions (interrupting briefly to ask a question), *shitsurei itashimasu* or *sumimasen* are more appropriate. Reaching for the deepest apology for every small moment dilutes its meaning when one needs it


## Example Sentences

<div class="keigo-card blue">
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>誠に申し訳ございません。ご不便をおかけいたしました。</code></span><br>
<em>Makoto ni moushiwake gozaimasen. Go-fuben wo o-kake itashimashita.</em><br>
I am truly very sorry. We have caused you inconvenience.
<br><br>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>申し訳ございません。ただいますぐに対応させていただきます。</code></span><br>
<em>Moushiwake gozaimasen. Tadaima sugu ni taiō sasete itadakimasu.</em><br>
I am very sorry. I will address this for you immediately.
<br><br>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>お待たせいたしまして、誠に申し訳ございません。</code></span><br>
<em>O-matase itashimashite, makoto ni moushiwake gozaimasen.</em><br>
I am truly very sorry for making you wait.
</div>

## Related Expressions

<div class="keigo-card blue">
<span class="keigo-badge info">すみません (sumimasen)</span> A lighter, more casual apology/excuse me; not appropriate for serious situations
<br><br>
<span class="keigo-badge info">失礼いたします (shitsurei itashimasu)</span> "Excuse me" for minor social intrusions, not for genuine errors
<br><br>
<span class="keigo-badge info">誠に申し訳ございません (makoto ni moushiwake gozaimasen)</span> Intensified form with *makoto ni* (truly): used when maximum sincerity is required
</div>

## Common Mistakes

<div class="keigo-card blue">
The most serious mistake is using *moushiwake gozaimasen* too casually, turning it into a routine phrase delivered without genuine feeling. When the deepest apology is required, guests can tell the difference between sincerity and performance. A second mistake is following the apology immediately with an explanation or excuse. Explanations should come *after* the guest's emotion has been acknowledged, never as a counter to it. The sequence matters enormously.
</div>

## Quiz

**A guest discovers their reserved room was given to another guest and is not available. What is the appropriate first response?**

- A. すみません、少し待ってください。(Sorry, please wait a moment: casual)
- B. 申し訳ございません。ただいますぐに対応いたします。(I am very sorry. I will resolve this immediately.)
- C. こちらの手違いではございません。(This is not our error.)
- D. 失礼いたします。(Excuse me.)

**Correct Answer:** 申し訳ございません。ただいますぐに対応いたします。(I am very sorry. I will resolve this immediately.)

**Explanation:** Losing a guest's reserved room is a serious failure that calls for the deepest apology in Japanese hospitality. *Moushiwake gozaimasen* conveys genuine responsibility without deflection, and pairing it immediately with a commitment to resolve shows the guest that words and action are inseparable. Lighter phrases like *sumimasen* or *shitsurei itashimasu* are far too mild for a situation this significant.
