title: どうぞ (douzo)
---

**どうぞ** is a notably versatile word in Japanese hospitality. Its core meaning is "please" or "go ahead," but its nuance shifts depending on the moment. When presenting an item to a guest, どうぞ means "here one is." When inviting a guest to enter a space, it means "please come in." When offering a seat, it means "please sit." When yielding at a door, it means "please, after you." In each case, どうぞ is the gentle hand extended toward the guest: an invitation without pressure.

The word almost always comes with a physical gesture. Presenting an item with both hands, gesturing toward a seat, opening a door, the gesture and the word work together, and one without the other feels incomplete. In Japanese hospitality, the physical act of offering carries just as much meaning as the words. A どうぞ delivered while looking the other direction, or with a single hand, loses most of its warmth.

どうぞ can also stand completely alone as a full utterance. Handing over a key, presenting a menu, offering a warm drink, in many of these moments the single word carries the full weight of the exchange without needing elaboration. That simplicity is part of its elegance. There are also moments where どうぞ alone is not quite enough, and a more specific phrase serves the guest better

## Related Expressions

| Expression | Description |
|---|---|
| こちらへどうぞ<br>kochira e dōzo | "Please come this way." どうぞ combined with a clear direction. Use this to invite a guest to follow one somewhere. |
| ごゆっくりどうぞ<br>goyukkuri dōzo | "Please take their time / relax." どうぞ paired with an invitation to ease. Particularly important when showing a guest to their room or presenting a meal. |
| どうぞ、お構いなく<br>dōzo, o-kamainaku | "Please don't worry on my behalf." Used when the guest is being overly considerate about not wanting to trouble one. |

## Common Mistakes

A common mistake is handing something to a guest without any verbal accompaniment, or saying どうぞ while distracted, without making eye contact or using both hands. The word works because of the full gesture it accompanies, and the physical and verbal need to arrive together. Another mistake is reaching for どうぞ in situations that call for a more specific phrase. Saying どうぞ alone when admitting a guest to their room, for example, feels slightly incomplete. 失礼いたします、ご案内いたします is more appropriate there, and shows the guest that the staff member is actively guiding, not just waving them through.

## Quiz

**You are handing a guest their room key card at the end of check-in. What is the most natural accompanying phrase?**

- A. お待ちください。(Please wait.)
- B. こちらがお部屋の鍵でございます。どうぞ。(Here is your room key. Please.)
- C. かしこまりました。(Certainly.)
- D. 失礼いたします。(Excuse me.)

**Correct Answer:** こちらがお部屋の鍵でございます。どうぞ。(Here is your room key. Please.)

**Explanation:** Naming the item before offering it gives the guest a clear moment of orientation, and どうぞ then extends it with warmth rather than pushing something across the desk. The other options belong to different moments in the interaction: asking the guest to wait, confirming a request, or signalling an intrusion have no place at the point of handover.
