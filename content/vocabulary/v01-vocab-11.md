**Pronunciation:** dō-zo — two syllables; the first is a long *ō* sound, do not shorten it to *do*

**Register:** Polite — *dōzo* is versatile and appropriate in most professional contexts. It is warm without being overly formal, and it works across a wide range of offering and inviting situations.

**どうぞ** is one of the most versatile words in Japanese hospitality. Its core meaning is "please" or "go ahead," but its nuance shifts depending on the situation. When presenting an item to a guest, *dōzo* means "here you are." When inviting a guest to enter a space, it means "please come in." When offering a seat, it means "please sit." When yielding at a door, it means "please, after you." In each case, *dōzo* is the gentle hand extended toward the guest — an invitation without pressure.

The word is almost always accompanied by a physical gesture: presenting an item with both hands, gesturing toward a seat, opening a door. The gesture and the word work together; one without the other feels incomplete. In Japanese hospitality, the physical act of giving or offering is as important as the verbal acknowledgement.

*Dōzo* can also stand alone as a complete utterance. In many situations — handing over a key, presenting a menu, offering a drink — the word carries the full weight of the exchange without needing elaboration. Its simplicity is part of its elegance.

## Example Sentences

**1.** こちらがお部屋の鍵でございます。どうぞ。
*Kochira ga o-heya no kagi de gozaimasu. Dōzo.*
"Here is your room key. Please."

**2.** こちらのお席にどうぞ。
*Kochira no o-seki ni dōzo.*
"Please take this seat."

**3.** 温かいお飲み物でございます。どうぞ、ごゆっくりどうぞ。
*Atatakai o-nomimono de gozaimasu. Dōzo, goyukkuri dōzo.*
"Here is a warm drink for you. Please, take your time and enjoy."

## Related Expressions

- **こちらへどうぞ (kochira e dōzo)** — "Please come this way" — *dōzo* combined with a direction
- **ごゆっくりどうぞ (goyukkuri dōzo)** — "Please take your time / relax" — *dōzo* combined with an invitation to ease
- **どうぞ、お構いなく (dōzo, o-kamainaku)** — "Please don't worry on my behalf" — used when the recipient is being too considerate

## Common Mistakes

A common mistake is handing something to a guest without any verbal accompaniment — or saying *dōzo* while distracted, without making eye contact or using both hands for the presentation. The word works because of the full gesture it accompanies. Another mistake is overusing *dōzo* in situations that require a more specific phrase — for example, *dōzo* when admitting a guest to their room feels slightly incomplete; *shitsurei itashimasu, go-annai itashimasu* (excuse me, I will show you in) is more appropriate.

## Quiz

**You are handing a guest their room key card at the end of check-in. What is the most natural accompanying phrase?**

- A. お待ちください。(Please wait.)
- B. こちらがお部屋の鍵でございます。どうぞ。(Here is your room key. Please.)
- C. かしこまりました。(Certainly.)
- D. 失礼いたします。(Excuse me.)

**Correct Answer:** こちらがお部屋の鍵でございます。どうぞ。(Here is your room key. Please.)

**Explanation:** *Dōzo* paired with a clear identification of the item being presented (*kochira ga o-heya no kagi de gozaimasu*) is the natural, warm way to complete a handover. The identification gives the guest context; *dōzo* extends the item with warmth. Present the key with both hands.
