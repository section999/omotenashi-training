title: 恐れ入ります (osore irimasu)
---
恐れ入ります is difficult to pin down in English because it does two different jobs depending on context. Literally it means something like "I enter into awe," with 恐れ (恐れ) carrying the sense of reverence or humble embarrassment, and *irimasu* meaning "to enter." Use it before making a request of a guest, and one is saying "I am so sorry to impose on you." Use it to receive a guest's gratitude, and one is saying "I am deeply honoured." The same phrase, two directions, one core idea: the speaker is small, the guest is honoured.

The key thing to understand is that 恐れ入ります is always about humbling oneself relative to the other person. When staff say it before asking a guest's name, one is acknowledging that even asking is a small imposition, and one is lowering oneself before doing so. This phrase sits above both すみません and 失礼いたします on the formality scale. Relying only on すみません means missing an entire register. It is like knowing "sorry" in English but not knowing "I beg their pardon" or "please forgive the intrusion."

恐れ入ります takes time to feel natural. When one first starts using it, it can feel theatrical, and that awkwardness shows. It should be practiced out loud until it flows without hesitation. The phrase works best when paired with genuine body language: a small, sincere bow and steady eye contact


## Example Sentences

**恐れ入りますが、お名前をもう一度お聞かせいただけますでしょうか。**  
*Osore irimasu ga, o-namae wo mō ichido o-kikase itadakemasu deshō ka.*  
I am terribly sorry to trouble you, but could I ask your name once more?

**恐れ入ります。ただいまご確認いたします。**  
*Osore irimasu. Tadaima go-kakunin itashimasu.*  
Thank you: excuse me for a moment while I confirm.

**恐れ入りますが、少々お待ちいただけますでしょうか。**  
*Osore irimasu ga, shōshō o-machi itadakemasu deshō ka.*  
I am terribly sorry to trouble you, but could you wait just a moment?

## Related Expressions

| Expression | Description |
|---|---|
| 失礼いたします<br>shitsurei itashimasu | "Excuse me", similar function but one step below 恐れ入ります in formality; this is the phrase staff will reach for most often in everyday professional situations, and that is completely fine |
| すみません<br>sumimasen | the everyday excuse me or apology that works fine among colleagues, but feels too casual for the register that 恐れ入ります signals to guests |
| とんでもございません<br>tondemo gozaimasen | "Not at all / one is too kind", the natural companion phrase when a guest praises one or their service; it deflects rather than accepts |

## Common Mistakes

The most common mistake is not using 恐れ入ります at all. A common error is defaulting to すみません even in elevated settings where the more refined phrase would be more fitting. The register of their language is part of the service. If the lobby is marble and the uniforms are tailored, their words should match. A second mistake is delivering 恐れ入ります in a flat or distracted tone. It should always come with genuine feeling and a small bow, not as a throwaway opener.

## Quiz

**A guest thanks you enthusiastically for your help. How do you respond?**

- A. どういたしまして。(You're welcome: too casual for formal hospitality)
- B. 恐れ入ります。お役に立てて光栄でございます。(I am most grateful. It is an honour to be of service.)
- C. いいえ。(No / not at all: too abrupt)
- D. ありがとうございます。(Thank you: receiving the thanks rather than deflecting it)

**Correct Answer:** 恐れ入ります。お役に立てて光栄でございます。(I am most grateful. It is an honour to be of service.)

**Explanation:** Receiving a guest's thanks with 恐れ入ります deflects the praise through humility rather than accepting it directly, which would risk sounding self-congratulatory. Pairing it with お役に立てて光栄でございます then elevates the guest by expressing genuine honour at having been of service, so their thanks land as something meaningful rather than a social formality.
