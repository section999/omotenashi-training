title: 冷蔵庫 (reizouko)
---
冷蔵庫 breaks down as 冷蔵 (refrigerated storage) and 庫 (storehouse), and it refers to the in-room refrigerator found in most Japanese hotels and many ryokan. In many hotel rooms it doubles as a minibar, pre-stocked with beverages and snacks that are individually priced and tracked. That setup is a common source of confusion at checkout if guests were not informed upfront which items cost money, and this kind of dispute is avoidable with one clear sentence during check-in. In business hotels or ryokan without minibar stock, the 冷蔵庫 is empty and free for the guest to use.

During room orientation, the guideline is clear: if the fridge is empty, 「冷蔵庫はご自由にお使いください」 is the appropriate phrase; if it is a minibar, 「冷蔵庫の飲み物はすべて有料となっております」 should be used. Guests who arrive with medications or special dietary items depend on knowing the refrigerator is available, so staff should mention it even when it seems routine. If a guest asks about ice, staff should let them know the ice machines are on each floor: 「製氷機は各階にございます」\n## Example Sentences

**1.** 冷蔵庫はご自由にお使いください。
*Reizouko wa go-jiyuu ni o-tsukai kudasai.*
"Please feel free to use the refrigerator."

**2.** 冷蔵庫内の飲み物はすべて有料となっております。
*Reizouko nai no nomimono wa subete yuuryou to natte orimasu.*
"All beverages inside the refrigerator are chargeable."

**3.** お薬など冷蔵が必要なものがございましたら、冷蔵庫をご利用ください。
*Okusuri nado reizou ga hitsuyou na mono ga gozaimashitara, reizouko wo go-riyou kudasai.*
"If you have medications or other items requiring refrigeration, please use the refrigerator."


## Related Expressions

- **ミニバー (minibaa)**: the English loanword used when the 冷蔵庫 is pre-stocked as a minibar. A ミニバー means everything inside is priced and tracked; an empty 冷蔵庫 is free for guests to use. Making this distinction clear avoids billing disputes.
- **製氷機 (seihouki)**: ice machine, typically in the corridor on each floor. When guests want ice for their 冷蔵庫 drinks, point them here, and knowing this term lets one gives a complete, helpful answer.


## Common Mistakes

The most frequent error is pointing to the 冷蔵庫 and saying 「冷蔵庫はこちらです」 without explaining the minibar situation. That leaves guests uncertain, which can lead to disputes at checkout. Using 「使えます」 (plain potential form) rather than the appropriate phrase 「ご自由にお使いください」 is another common lapse. Skipping the 冷蔵庫 orientation entirely is the most significant oversight; guests with medications requiring refrigeration may discover later that they were not informed about the refrigerator.

## Quiz

**Guest asks: "Can I put my drinks in the fridge?" Which is most appropriate?**

- A. 冷蔵庫はご自由にお使いください (please feel free to use the refrigerator)
- B. 冷蔵庫使えません (cannot use the refrigerator)
- C. 冷蔵庫どうぞ (please use the refrigerator)
- D. 冷蔵庫の食品はすべて有料となっております (all refrigerator items are charged)

**Correct Answer:** 冷蔵庫はご自由にお使いください (please feel free to use the refrigerator)

**Explanation:** 冷蔵庫はご自由にお使いください warmly grants permission in a way that is both polite and immediately clear. Saying it cannot be used contradicts standard hotel practice, どうぞ alone is too abrupt for a professional response, and leading with a minibar charge statement misrepresents the situation when the guest wants to store their own drinks.
