**Season:** Spring · March — May

Japan's business and educational calendar resets on April 1st. Companies begin new fiscal years, universities welcome new students, and employees transfer to new positions. This creates a wave of celebratory travel — family trips to accompany children starting university, farewell dinners, and welcome gatherings.

**Hospitality context:** Many guests are experiencing significant life transitions. A warm, congratulatory tone is appropriate. Listen for context cues: a family checking in may be accompanying a child moving to the city. A solo guest in business attire may be relocating for a new position.

---

**Celebratory Greeting — New Enrollment**

ご入学おめでとうございます。

*Go-nyuugaku omedetou gozaimasu.*

"Congratulations on your enrollment."

---

**Celebratory Greeting — New Position**

ご就職おめでとうございます。

*Go-shuushoku omedetou gozaimasu.*

"Congratulations on your new position."

---

## Quiz

**A family checks in on April 2nd with a nervous-looking young adult carrying boxes. What tone is most appropriate?**

- A. Businesslike and efficient — process the check-in quickly
- B. Warm and congratulatory — acknowledge the life transition
- C. Formal and distant — maintain professional neutrality
- D. Ask directly why they are moving

**Correct Answer:** Warm and congratulatory — acknowledge the life transition

**Explanation:** April 1st marks Japan's new academic and fiscal year. A family with a young adult and belongings is likely accompanying a new university student. A warm, celebratory tone aligned with this context is an expression of omoiyari.
