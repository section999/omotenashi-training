**Season:** Winter · December — February

New Year is Japan's most important cultural period. December 31st (Oomisoka) is spent with family, and January 1–3 are the core holiday days. Many Japanese visit shrines and temples for Hatsumode (first shrine visit of the year). Ryokan and hot spring resorts are booked months in advance.

**Hospitality context:** New Year's greetings change precisely on January 1st. Using the correct greeting at the right time is a mark of professionalism. Osechi ryori (traditional New Year cuisine) is central to the experience at traditional properties. Properties often decorate entrances with kadomatsu (bamboo and pine arrangements) and shimekazari (rope decorations).

- Before January 1: use year-end farewell expressions
- From January 1: switch to New Year greeting immediately
- New Year greetings remain appropriate through mid-January (松の内 matsu no uchi period)

---

**New Year Greeting (from January 1)**

明けましておめでとうございます。本年もよろしくお願いいたします。

*Akemashite omedetou gozaimasu. Honnen mo yoroshiku onegai itashimasu.*

"Happy New Year. I look forward to your continued support this year."

---

**Year-End Farewell (December)**

良いお年をお迎えください。

*Yoi otoshi wo omukae kudasai.*

"Please have a good New Year." *(Said before the New Year arrives.)*

---

## Quiz

**A guest checks in on December 31st. Which greeting is correct?**

- A. 明けましておめでとうございます (Happy New Year)
- B. 良いお年をお迎えください (Please have a good New Year)
- C. ありがとうございます (Thank you)
- D. No seasonal greeting is necessary

**Correct Answer:** 良いお年をお迎えください (Please have a good New Year)

**Explanation:** The New Year greeting 明けましておめでとうございます is only used from January 1st onward. Before the New Year arrives, the correct farewell is 良いお年をお迎えください. Using the right greeting at the right moment is a mark of professionalism.
