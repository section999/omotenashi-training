**Season:** Summer · June — August

Obon is Japan's most significant ancestral remembrance period. Many Japanese travel to their family hometowns to tend graves and hold family reunions. Urban hotels may see a drop in occupancy while rural and coastal properties fill completely. Traditional festivals (bon odori dance gatherings) take place throughout the country.

**Hospitality context:** The emotional register of Obon is reflective and familial — different from the celebratory energy of other peak seasons. Some guests may be processing grief. Quieter, more attentive service is appropriate. Yukata are common; traditional-style properties should be fully stocked.

- Domestic travel peaks in second week of August — prepare accordingly
- Be aware guests may be in a reflective, not festive, frame of mind
- Public transport and roads are very busy — airport transfers may take longer

## Quiz

**During Obon, a guest seems quiet and subdued compared to typical holiday guests. How should you adjust your service?**

- A. Encourage them to join the hotel's lively evening activities
- B. Provide quieter, more attentive service and avoid an overly festive tone
- C. Ask them directly if they are grieving
- D. Treat them exactly the same as any other guest

**Correct Answer:** Provide quieter, more attentive service and avoid an overly festive tone

**Explanation:** Obon carries a reflective, familial emotional register. Some guests may be travelling to remember ancestors or support family. Reading the guest's emotional state and adjusting your energy accordingly is a fundamental omotenashi skill.
