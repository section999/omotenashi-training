---

**Season:** Spring · March — May

Golden Week is Japan's longest holiday cluster — a series of national holidays concentrated over approximately ten days. Showa Day (April 29), Constitution Day (May 3), Greenery Day (May 4), and Children's Day (May 5) fall in quick succession. Japan's tourism infrastructure operates at maximum capacity.

**Hospitality context:** Hotels are fully booked months in advance. Guests are in holiday mode — relaxed, indulgent, and expecting premium experiences. Domestic travel is very high, meaning many guests will be Japanese families. Manage queues with grace, maintain energy among staff, and ensure all amenities are fully stocked.

- Staff levels should be at maximum during Golden Week
- Pre-anticipate breakfast and dining demand — consider extended service hours
- Children's Day (May 5): families with children are the dominant guest type

## Quiz

**During Golden Week, a long queue forms at the front desk. What is the priority?**

- A. Ask guests to come back later when it is less busy
- B. Close one desk to consolidate staff
- C. Manage the queue with grace, maintain energy, and ensure all guests feel acknowledged
- D. Speed through check-ins without greeting guests

**Correct Answer:** Manage the queue with grace, maintain energy, and ensure all guests feel acknowledged

**Explanation:** Golden Week operates at maximum capacity. Guests are in holiday mode and expect premium service even during busy periods. Maintaining warmth and energy under pressure is a core omotenashi skill.
