---

**Season:** Winter · December — February

In winter, acknowledging the cold in your greeting is a natural and considerate act — particularly when guests have come from outside. It signals awareness and care.

---

**Cold-Weather Greeting**

寒い中をお越しいただきありがとうございます。

*Samui naka wo okoshi itadaki arigatou gozaimasu.*

"Thank you for coming in this cold."

---

**Written Winter Greeting (formal)**

寒中お見舞い申し上げます。

*Kanchuu omimai moushiagemasu.*

"I offer my midwinter greetings to you."

---

## Quiz

**A guest arrives in January looking cold after walking from the station. What is the most appropriate greeting?**

- A. Begin the check-in process immediately without comment
- B. Acknowledge the cold warmly: "Thank you for coming in this cold" and offer a warm drink if available
- C. Ask the guest why they did not take a taxi
- D. Comment that the cold is unusual for this time of year

**Correct Answer:** Acknowledge the cold warmly: "Thank you for coming in this cold" and offer a warm drink if available

**Explanation:** In Japanese hospitality, acknowledging weather conditions is a meaningful act of care. Saying 寒い中をお越しいただきありがとうございます — combined with a warm drink offer — communicates that the guest's comfort is your genuine concern.
