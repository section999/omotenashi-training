**Season:** Autumn · September, November

**Keirō no Hi (敬老の日)**, Respect for the Aged Day, is a national holiday observed on the third Monday of September, dedicated to honouring Japan's elderly population and celebrating their contributions to society. Many families use this day to take older relatives on trips, organise celebratory meals, or visit property together that may otherwise be difficult to access. Multi-generational groups, grandparents, adult children, and grandchildren, are the defining guest profile of this period.

**The cultural context:**

Japan has one of the oldest populations in the world, and the treatment of the elderly is taken seriously as a social and ethical matter. *Omoiyari* (思いやり), empathetic consideration for others, is particularly significant toward older guests, for whom travel may require greater effort and planning than it once did. The willingness of a family to organise a special trip for an elderly relative is itself a meaningful act; the hospitality professional's role is to honour the occasion.

**Physical and practical considerations:**

Elderly guests often have specific needs that may not be communicated directly:
- **Mobility**: Longer walking distances, steps, and hard-to-access amenities may present challenges. Know where the lifts are, know which routes avoid stairs, and offer guidance proactively without drawing attention to any limitation.
- **Pace**: Service should slow to match the guest's pace, do not rush check-in, do not move ahead of an elderly guest.
- **Communication**: Speak clearly, at a moderate pace, facing the guest. Do not redirect communication to accompanying family members unless the elderly guest signals this preference.
- **Diet**: Traditional Japanese cuisine is generally well-suited to elderly guests, but confirm dietary requirements and any difficulty with particular textures or temperatures.

**The acknowledgement opportunity:**

A small gesture from the property specifically directed at the elderly guest, a seasonal flower, a personal note, a small seasonal sweet, communicates that the property recognises the significance of the occasion. This is not about the cost of the gesture; it is about the recognition that this person and this day have been noticed.

## Seasonal Greeting

**When addressing an elderly guest:**
いつまでも、お元気でいらっしゃいますことを心よりお祈り申し上げます。

*Itsumade mo, o-genki de irasshaimasu koto wo kokoro yori o-inori mōshiagemasu.*

"We sincerely wish you the very best of health for many years to come."

**To accompanying family:**
ご家族でのお越し、誠にありがとうございます。

*Go-kazoku de no o-koshi, makoto ni arigatō gozaimasu.*

"Thank you sincerely for visiting as a family."

## Quiz

**A family of five arrives on Respect for the Aged Day. The grandmother is clearly the honoured guest: the family is attentive and ceremonious around her. She moves slowly and uses a walking stick. How should the staff respond?**

- A. Direct all communication to the adult child who is managing the booking, as they are the primary contact
- B. Address the grandmother directly and warmly as the primary guest; proactively offer accessible routing, an unhurried pace, and any practical assistance; consider a small personal acknowledgement of the occasion
- C. Treat the check-in as standard: any extra consideration might embarrass the grandmother by drawing attention to her age
- D. Ask the family if the grandmother needs any special assistance before assuming anything

**Correct Answer:** Address the grandmother directly and warmly as the primary guest; proactively offer accessible routing, an unhurried pace, and any practical assistance; consider a small personal acknowledgement of the occasion

**Explanation:** On Keirō no Hi, the elderly guest is the honoured person. Directing communication to them: not around them to accompanying family: is a fundamental act of respect. Proactive physical assistance and a slowed service pace honour their dignity; a small gesture of acknowledgement makes the occasion real.
