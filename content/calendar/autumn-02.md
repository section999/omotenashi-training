title: Respect for the Aged Day (敬老の日) — 3rd Monday of September
---

**Season:** Autumn · September — November

This national holiday honours Japan's elderly population. Many families take older relatives out for meals or short trips. Elderly guests should always receive additional attentiveness — but on this day especially, multi-generational groups are common and a celebratory dynamic may be present.

**Hospitality context:** Anticipate the needs of elderly guests without drawing attention to their age. Offer to carry bags, slow your pace when walking with them, provide clear and simple directions. If a family is celebrating an elderly relative, a small acknowledgement from the property is a meaningful touch.

## Quiz

**A multi-generational family arrives on Respect for the Aged Day. The elderly grandparent appears to be the focus of the occasion. What is the ideal approach?**

- A. Direct all communication to the youngest adult to ensure efficiency
- B. Address the grandparent with extra warmth and attentiveness; consider a small acknowledgement from the property
- C. Treat the visit as a standard check-in without acknowledging the occasion
- D. Ask the family what the occasion is before adjusting your service

**Correct Answer:** Address the grandparent with extra warmth and attentiveness; consider a small acknowledgement from the property

**Explanation:** On Respect for the Aged Day, multi-generational families often travel to celebrate an elderly relative. Directing warmth toward the grandparent — and offering a small gesture of acknowledgement — reflects cultural awareness and genuine hospitality.
