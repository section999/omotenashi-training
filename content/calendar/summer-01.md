**Season:** Summer · June — August

The rainy season (tsuyu) brings sustained humidity and frequent rain across most of Japan. Guests arrive damp, and the atmosphere can feel heavy. This is an underrated opportunity for warmth.

**Hospitality context:** Have umbrellas available at entrances. Greet guests who have clearly walked in the rain with immediate acknowledgement. A warm towel or dry cloth offered without being asked is a powerful act of omoiyari.

---

**Wet-Weather Greeting**

お足元の悪い中、お越しいただきありがとうございます。

*O-ashimoto no warui naka, okosei itadaki arigatou gozaimasu.*

"Thank you for coming despite the difficult conditions underfoot."

---

## Quiz

**A guest arrives visibly wet from heavy rain. What is the ideal first response?**

- A. Complete the check-in process before addressing the guest's comfort
- B. Immediately offer a warm towel and acknowledge the difficult conditions
- C. Comment on the weather without taking any action
- D. Wait for the guest to ask for assistance

**Correct Answer:** Immediately offer a warm towel and acknowledge the difficult conditions

**Explanation:** Anticipating a guest's need before they ask is the essence of omoiyari. During tsuyu, offering a warm towel without being prompted — paired with a wet-weather greeting — is a powerful expression of genuine hospitality.
