A hotel operates as a network of departments that depend on each other to deliver seamless guest service. The front desk coordinates with housekeeping, maintenance, the restaurant, and the concierge dozens of times each day. The quality of these cross-department exchanges directly determines how quickly guest needs are met.

Cross-department communication uses professional but functional language, not the full guest-facing keigo register, but not casual speech either. The goal is accurate, fast coordination between colleagues who share a service mission.

## The Cross-Department Communication Standard

- **1. Identify yourself and your department first:** The receiving department needs to know immediately who is calling and from where.
- **2. State the guest and room number before the request:** The receiving department will need this to act. Give it before the detail, not after.
- **3. Confirm the request was received and will be handled:** Never end a cross-department call without a clear acknowledgment from the other side.

## Quiz

**When making a cross-department call, what should you state first?**

- A. The guest's name
- B. The specific request or problem
- C. Your name and department
- D. The room number

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The cross-department communication standard requires identifying yourself and your department first. The receiving department needs to know immediately who is calling and from where before they can properly process any request. The room number and guest information come next, followed by the specific request. This order ensures that if the call is interrupted or the line is unclear, the receiving department at least knows the source of the communication and can call back.
