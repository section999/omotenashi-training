title: Why Internal Communication Requires Its Own Language
---

Hotel Japanese has two distinct modes: guest-facing and internal. Most training focuses on guest-facing keigo, but the language staff use with each other, with supervisors, colleagues, and other departments, follows a different set of rules that is equally important to professional service.

Internal communication errors do not stay internal. A vague report to a supervisor leads to a delayed response. A request that sounds casual from a junior to a senior creates friction. A cross-department message missing key information results in a guest waiting longer than necessary. The quality of internal language directly determines the quality of guest outcomes.

## Two Modes, Different Rules

<div class="keigo-card blue">

| Dimension | Guest-Facing | Internal |
|---|---|---|
| Register | Highest (尊敬語 + 謙譲語) | Formal but functional |
| Priority | Guest comfort and emotional experience | Accuracy and efficiency |
| Length | As warm and complete as needed | Concise; key facts first |
| Apology style | Deeply softened, emotionally attentive | Direct acknowledgment, solution-focused |
| Goal | Create the feeling of being cared for | Enable fast, accurate action |

</div>

## The ホウレンソウ Framework

Japanese professional culture structures internal communication around three types: 報告 (houkoku), 連絡 (renraku), and 相談 (soudan). Together they form ホウレンソウ (horenso), named after spinach, and represent the three communication duties every staff member has.

<div class="keigo-card blue">
<span class="keigo-badge info">報告 (Reporting)</span> Informing a supervisor of the outcome of a task, a problem discovered, or a guest situation requiring attention. This flows upward.<br><br>
<span class="keigo-badge info">連絡 (Informing)</span> Passing factual information to colleagues or departments who need it. This flows horizontally and in any direction.<br><br>
<span class="keigo-badge info">相談 (Consulting)</span> Bringing an uncertain situation or decision to a supervisor or experienced colleague and asking for guidance. This flows upward.
</div>

All three require different language structures, different timing, and different levels of formality. The three lessons that follow cover each in depth.

## The Hierarchy and What It Means for Language

Japanese workplaces operate with a clear hierarchical structure that directly shapes language. The senpai-kohai (先輩後輩) system, senior colleague and junior colleague, determines register choices even between peers of similar rank.

<div class="keigo-card blue">
<span class="keigo-badge info">Junior to senior (後輩 to 先輩 / staff to supervisor)</span><br>
Use formal verb endings, polite request forms, and respectful framing. Avoid casual contractions or sentence-final particles like ね and よ.<br><br>
<span class="keigo-badge info">Peer to peer (同僚)</span><br>
Standard polite forms are appropriate (ます/です). Full casual speech is not appropriate in a professional hotel context even between friends at work.<br><br>
<span class="keigo-badge info">Senior to junior (先輩 to 後輩)</span><br>
Seniors may use plain forms when giving instructions, but should model the professional register expected of the junior.
</div>

## Why Internal Language Matters for Guests

<div class="keigo-card blue">
A front desk staff member discovers a guest's room has a maintenance issue. The quality of the next 15 minutes depends entirely on internal communication:<br><br>
<strong>Poor internal chain:</strong> Vague report to supervisor → supervisor unsure of urgency → delayed response → guest waits → guest dissatisfied<br><br>
<strong>Strong internal chain:</strong> Clear report with room number, issue type, and guest status → supervisor acts immediately → issue resolved before guest returns → guest never knows there was a problem<br><br>
The guest's experience of the hotel is the sum of all the internal communication that happened before they ever interacted with a staff member.
</div>

## Key Phrases

<div class="keigo-card blue">
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>ご報告があります</code></span>: I have something to report.<br><br>

## Quiz

**A junior staff member needs to ask their supervisor for advice on handling an unusual guest request. Which ホウレンソウ type is this?**

- A. 報告: because it involves a guest situation
- B. 連絡: because information is being passed between two people
- C. 相談: because the staff member is seeking guidance on an uncertain situation
- D. None: internal communication does not require a specific framework

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** 相談 (consulting) applies when a staff member brings an uncertain situation or decision to a supervisor and asks for guidance. This differs from 報告 (reporting completed facts) and 連絡 (passing known information). The staff member does not yet know the answer; they are seeking it. Recognizing which type of communication is needed determines the correct language structure and the appropriate moment to initiate it.
