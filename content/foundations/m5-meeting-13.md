title: Common Mindset Failures
---

Most handover errors are not caused by lack of knowledge. They are caused by treating the handover as secondary to other tasks. This lesson names the four most common mindset failures and how to fix each one.

## Failure 1: Rushing

Writing the handover in the last two minutes of the shift guarantees missing information. Under time pressure, early-shift events are forgotten, status labels are guessed rather than checked, and pending items lose their owners.

**Fix:** Update the notes continuously throughout the shift, not all at once at the end. When a complaint is received, record it then. When a request is resolved, update the status then. End-of-shift notes should only be a final check to ensure nothing has been missed.

## Failure 2: Assuming the Next Person Already Knows

"They'll figure it out" is not a professional position. The next person was not there. They did not hear the guest's tone of voice, did not know that Room 305 called twice, and do not know that the repair was promised for "sometime tomorrow morning."

**Fix:** Write as if the reader has no background knowledge of the shift. Every entry should be self-contained.

## Failure 3: Emotional Filtering

Writing more about how you felt during a difficult interaction than about what happened and what needs to happen next. Entries like 「かなり大変なクレームでした」 or 「対応が難しかったです」 describe the writer's experience, not the operational situation.

**Fix:** Read the entry before submitting and remove any word that describes your feelings. If a word does not describe a fact, a status, or an action, it does not belong in a handover note.

## Failure 4: Leaving Tasks Without Owners

"To be confirmed" with no name assigned is the most common source of tasks falling through between shifts. When both shifts assume the other is responsible, the task is never done.

**Fix:** Every unresolved item must have a specific name or department attached. 確認待ち alone is not complete; フロント確認待ち is.

## Quiz

**A staff member finishes their shift notes at the end of the day and writes everything from memory. What professional standard are they failing to meet?**

- A. Notes must be written in the morning only
- B. Handover notes should be updated continuously throughout the shift so no detail is forgotten
- C. Only managers are allowed to write handover notes at the end of a shift
- D. Memory-based notes are acceptable if they are detailed enough

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Writing all handover notes from memory at the end of a shift almost guarantees incomplete or inaccurate entries. Details from early in the shift are easily forgotten under the pressure of a busy day. The professional standard is to update notes continuously as events occur. End-of-shift notes should only be a final check to ensure nothing has been missed, not the primary documentation event.
