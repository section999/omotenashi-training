title: Irregular 謙譲語 Verbs
---

Like sonkeigo, the most important 謙譲語 verbs are irregular. They have completely new forms with no connection to the plain verb. These are the most frequently used humble forms in hotel service and must be memorized.

## Group 1: Irregular Kenjogo Verbs

| Plain Form | 謙譲語 Form | Meaning in Context |
|---|---|---|
| <span>行く</span> | <span>参ります</span> | I go (toward the guest) |
| <span>行く</span> | <span>伺います</span> | I visit / go humbly |
| <span>来る</span> | <span>参ります</span> | I come |
| <span>いる</span> | <span>おります</span> | I am here |
| <span>言う</span> | <span>申します</span> | I say |
| <span>する</span> | <span>いたします</span> | I do |
| <span>聞く / 訪ねる</span> | <span>伺います</span> | I ask / I visit |
| <span>もらう</span> | <span>いただきます</span> | I humbly receive |
| <span>見る</span> | <span>拝見します</span> | I look at / I read (humbly) |
| <span>知る</span> | <span>存じます</span> | I know |
| <span>食べる / 飲む</span> | <span>いただきます</span> | I humbly eat / drink |
| <span>あげる</span> | <span>差し上げます</span> | I give (to the guest) |

## 参ります vs 伺います

Both are humble forms of movement verbs, but their nuance differs.

| Form | Usage |
|---|---|
| <span>参ります</span> | General movement toward a place or person; "I will come / go" |
| <span>伺います</span> | Going to the guest's location, visiting, or humbly asking; carries a more deferential nuance |

<div class="keigo-card blue">
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>お部屋までお荷物をお持ちして参ります。</code></span><br>
<em>o-heya made o-nimotsu o o-mochi shite mairimasu.</em><br>
I will bring your luggage to your room.<br><br>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>明日またお部屋に伺います。</code></span><br>
<em>ashita mata o-heya ni ukagaimasu.</em><br>
I will visit your room again tomorrow.
</div>

## Quiz

**A staff member wants to say "I will come right away." Which is correct?**

- A. すぐ来ます。
- B. すぐいらっしゃいます。
- C. ただいま参ります。
- D. ただいまいただきます。

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** 参ります is the 謙譲語 form of 来る and 行く. ただいま (right now) combined with 参ります is the standard hotel phrase for "I will come right away." Option A uses the plain form 来ます with no humility. Option B uses いらっしゃいます, which is 尊敬語 for the guest's movement; applying it to the staff elevates them above the guest. Option D uses いただきます, which means "to humbly receive" and does not fit this context.
