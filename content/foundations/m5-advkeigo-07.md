title: Keigo and Gender-Neutral Language
---

Traditional Japanese has distinct masculine and feminine speech patterns, but modern hotel service demands gender-neutral keigo that treats all guests equally. Choosing neutral forms ensures professionalism and avoids assumptions based on gender.

```dialogue
Staff A:  本日のお客様、書類のご住所を拝見したところ、
          どちらもご夫妻でいらっしゃいますね。
          お連れ様のことを「奥様」とお呼びするのは
          適切でしょうか？
Staff B:  最近は「奥様」や「ご主人様」といった呼称は
          避けたほうが良い場合もあります。配偶者の方は
          「ご一緒のお客様」や「お連れ様」が無難です。
Staff A:  確かに、同性のカップルや未婚の方もいらっしゃいますもんね。
Staff B:  ええ。また、「〜だわ」「〜かしら」といった
          女性的な言葉遣いや、「〜だぞ」「〜かよ」といった
          男性的な言葉遣いは、ホテルでは使いません。
          常に中立で丁寧な「です・ます」が基本です。
Staff A:  なるほど。相手の性別に関係なく、同じ品質の
          敬語でお迎えするのがプロフェッショナルですね。
```

## Key Phrases

| Japanese | English | Usage Note |
|---|---|---|
| ご夫妻でいらっしゃいます | You are a married couple (honorific) | いらっしゃる elevates the subject; neutral and respectful |
| 奥様とお呼びするのは適切でしょうか | Would it be appropriate to call her okusama? | お呼びする is humble; 適切でしょうか softens the question for discussion |
| ご一緒のお客様 | The guest accompanying you | Gender-neutral alternative to 奥様 or ご主人様 |
| お連れ様 | Your companion | A neutral term that avoids gender assumptions |
| 同性のカップル | Same-sex couples | Acknowledges diverse guest compositions without judgment |
| 〜だわ、〜かしら | Feminine sentence endings | Traditional women's speech; inappropriate for professional service |
| 〜だぞ、〜かよ | Masculine sentence endings | Traditional men's speech; too rough for guest-facing roles |
| 中立で丁寧な「です・ます」 | Neutral and polite desu/masu | The standard register for professional hotel Japanese |

## Step-by-Step Breakdown

**Step 1: Use Gender-Neutral Terms for Relationships**

Instead of 奥様 (wife) or ご主人様 (husband), use ご一緒のお客様 or お連れ様. These phrases describe the relationship in terms of accompaniment rather than marital or gender roles, making them appropriate for all couples including same-sex partners.

**Step 2: Avoid Gendered Sentence Endings**

Traditional feminine endings such as 〜だわ, 〜かしら, 〜のよ and masculine endings such as 〜だぞ, 〜かよ, 〜ぜ have no place in hotel keigo. Stick to neutral です・ます endings supplemented by ございます for formality. This keeps the focus on the guest rather than the speaker's gender identity.

**Step 3: Use Neutral Honorific Prefixes Consistently**

Apply お and ご prefixes to all relevant nouns regardless of the guest's gender. お客様, ご予約, お部屋, お手荷物 are all gender-neutral. Do not use different vocabulary based on whether the guest appears male or female.

**Step 4: Ask About Preferences Without Assumption**

When offering services that involve titles or forms of address, ask neutrally. Phrases like お名前はどのようにお呼びすればよろしいでしょうか allow the guest to specify their preferred address without imposing assumptions.

**Step 5: Train the Entire Team on Inclusive Language**

Gender-neutral keigo is a team standard, not an individual choice. Ensure all staff members use consistent terminology for couples, groups, and individuals. Regular training updates help the team stay current with evolving social norms.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It Is Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| 奥様はどのようなご朝食になさいますか？ | Assumes the guest is married and male; may not reflect reality | ご一緒のお客様はどのようなご朝食になさいますか？ |
| 女の方のお名前をお願いします | Separating guests by gender is unnecessary and potentially offensive | お連れ様のお名前もお聞かせいただけますでしょうか |
| 〜かしら、お部屋はこちらですわ | Feminine ending sounds unprofessional in hotel service | お部屋はこちらでございます |
| お客さん、これいいっすね | Casual masculine speech with っす is inappropriate | お客様、こちらはいかがでございますか |

## Quiz

**What is the most appropriate way to refer to a guest's partner without assuming gender or marital status?**

- A. 奥様
- B. ご主人様
- C. ご一緒のお客様
- D. 彼女・彼氏

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** ご一緒のお客様 is a gender-neutral and marital-status-neutral term that respectfully refers to the person accompanying the guest. 奥様 and ご主人様 assume marital status and gender, while 彼女・彼氏 is too casual for hotel service.
