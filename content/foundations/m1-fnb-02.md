title: Handling Dietary Requirements
---

Dietary requirements — allergies, intolerances, religious restrictions, personal preferences — must be handled with absolute care, complete discretion, and genuine warmth. The guest should never feel that their requirement is an inconvenience, a complication, or an afterthought. The ideal response communicates that it has been anticipated, planned for, and handled without any need for concern on their part.

**The stakes of getting this wrong:**

A food allergy is not a preference. For a guest with a severe allergy — to nuts, shellfish, gluten, dairy — an error is not a disappointing meal. It is a medical emergency. The weight of this responsibility must be felt by every person in the service chain, from the front desk who records the allergy at check-in, to the kitchen team who prepares every course, to the server who delivers the plate.

The same applies to religious dietary restrictions. A guest who keeps halal or kosher is not being difficult; they are operating within a framework of personal integrity that deserves absolute respect.

**The disclosure and communication chain:**

The moment a dietary requirement is disclosed — whether at booking, at check-in, or at the table — the communication chain must begin immediately:

1. **Record it clearly and specifically** — "no nuts" must specify whether this includes nut-derived oils, traces, cross-contamination. "Vegetarian" must be clarified: does it include fish? Dairy? Eggs? Do not assume.

2. **Communicate immediately to the kitchen** — do not pass a note and assume it will be read. Confirm with the kitchen directly. For a serious allergy, confirm verbally with the chef or sous chef.

3. **Confirm back to the guest** — *"I've let the kitchen know about your allergy. Our chef will ensure that all courses are prepared without any nuts, including traces. Please let me know if you have any concerns about any particular dish."*

4. **Follow through every course** — at each dish, confirm that it meets the guest's requirement. Do not present a dish and say "I think this is fine." Know that it is.

**Making the alternative feel like an upgrade:**

When a guest with dietary restrictions receives a modified dish, the goal is that it should feel chosen — not substituted. A well-prepared vegetarian option presented with the same care as the standard menu item, explained with the same enthusiasm, served in the same quality of vessel, is not a lesser experience. It is a different one, prepared with equal care.

The guest should never feel lesser for their requirement. They should feel that the kitchen was glad to prepare something specifically for them.

## Key Phrases

**アレルギーの情報を厨房にお伝えしております。すべてのお料理でご対応いたします。** *(Arerugī no jōhō wo chūbō ni o-tsutae shite orimasu. Subete no o-ryōri de go-taiō itashimasu.)*
"I have passed your allergy information to the kitchen. We will accommodate it across all courses."

**こちらのお料理には○○は一切使用しておりません。** *(Kochira no o-ryōri ni wa ○○ wa issai shiyō shite orimasen.)*
"This dish contains absolutely no ○○." — the definitive confirmation, using *issai* (one bit, absolutely none) for clarity.

**他にお気をつけいただきたい食材はございますか？** *(Hoka ni o-ki wo tsukeitadakitai shokuzai wa gozaimasu ka?)*
"Are there any other ingredients you would like us to be careful about?" — asking proactively, rather than waiting for a problem to arise.

## Quiz

**A guest mentions at the table that they have a serious shellfish allergy. Two courses are already prepared in the kitchen. What is the correct sequence of actions?**

- A. Note the allergy and ensure the remaining courses avoid shellfish
- B. Stop service immediately, inform the kitchen of the allergy, confirm the status of all prepared courses with the chef, return to the guest with a specific confirmation of what has been checked and what will be adjusted, and follow through on every remaining course
- C. Ask the guest if they are sure about the allergy, in case it is a preference rather than a medical requirement
- D. Inform the manager and let them handle the communication with the kitchen

**Correct Answer:** Stop service immediately, inform the kitchen of the allergy, confirm the status of all prepared courses with the chef, return to the guest with a specific confirmation of what has been checked and what will be adjusted, and follow through on every remaining course

**Explanation:** A shellfish allergy is a medical safety issue. The correct response is immediate, thorough, and verified — not assumed. The guest needs a specific confirmation that the information has been received by the kitchen and that every course will be checked, not a general reassurance that "we'll take care of it."
