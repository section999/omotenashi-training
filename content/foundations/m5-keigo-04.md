The most important sonkeigo verbs are irregular, meaning they use completely different forms rather than following the standard sonkeigo patterns. Because these verbs appear frequently in hotel and customer-service interactions, they are essential to memorize.

## Group 1: Irregular Sonkeigo Verbs

| Plain Form | Sonkeigo Form | Context |
|---|---|---|
| <span>いる</span> | <span>いらっしゃいます</span> | Guest is present |
| <span>来る</span> | <span>いらっしゃいます</span> | Guest arrives |
| <span>行く</span> | <span>いらっしゃいます</span> | Guest goes |
| <span>食べる</span> | <span>召し上がります</span> | Guest eats |
| <span>飲む</span> | <span>召し上がります</span> | Guest drinks |
| <span>言う</span> | <span>おっしゃいます</span> | Guest says |
| <span>する</span> | <span>なさいます</span> | Guest does |
| <span>見る</span> | <span>ご覧になります</span> | Guest looks / sees |
| <span>知る</span> | <span>ご存知です</span> | Guest knows |
| <span>くれる</span> | <span>くださいます</span> | Guest gives |
| <span>休む</span> | <span>お休みになります</span> | Guest rests |

## One Verb, Three Meanings

いらっしゃいます is the sonkeigo form for いる, 来る, and 行く. Context determines which meaning is intended.

<div class="keigo-card yellow">
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>ロビーにいらっしゃいますか。</code></span><br>
<em>robii ni irasshaimasu ka.</em><br>
Are you in the lobby? (いる)<br><br>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>何時頃いらっしゃいますか。</code></span><br>
<em>nanji goro irasshaimasu ka.</em><br>
Around what time will you arrive? (来る)<br><br>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>レストランへいらっしゃいますか。</code></span><br>
<em>resutoran e irasshaimasu ka.</em><br>
Will you be going to the restaurant? (行く)
</div>

## Quiz

**A guest is looking at the menu. Which sentence correctly describes this in 尊敬語?**

- A. お客様がメニューを見ます。
- B. お客様がメニューをご覧になります。
- C. お客様がメニューを拝見します。
- D. お客様がメニューをご覧いたします。

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** 見る becomes ご覧になります in 尊敬語 when the guest is the subject. Option A uses the plain verb 見る with no sonkeigo. Option C uses 拝見します, which is 謙譲語 for the staff's humble looking; applying it to the guest lowers them. Option D uses ご覧いたします, mixing sonkeigo (ご覧) with kenjogo (いたします) in a grammatically incorrect combination.
