title: The Art of Recommendation
---

In Japanese hospitality, a recommendation is an act of care, not a sales tool. When a staff member suggests a dish, a wine pairing, or a menu change, it should feel like advice from someone who genuinely knows what they're talking about and wants the guest to have the best possible experience. It should never feel like an upsell.

The distinction comes down to motivation. A recommendation made in the guest's interest -- because you honestly believe they'll enjoy it -- lands differently from one made to increase the bill. Guests are perceptive. They notice whether your suggestion follows naturally from what they've told you, or whether it seems to have nothing to do with what they've expressed.

**What makes a recommendation trustworthy:**

To recommend effectively, you have to know the menu personally. Not just the descriptions -- the actual flavour profile, the texture, the preparation method, what it pairs with, and what kind of guest typically loves it. If you haven't tasted a dish, you can't recommend it with real honesty. If you've tasted it and it isn't to your preference, you can still recommend it accurately -- just be transparent: *"I personally lean toward lighter dishes, but many of our guests who enjoy ○○ find this one exceptional."*

A trustworthy recommendation:
- Follows from what the guest has already expressed or ordered
- Is specific: not "the fish is very good" but "the flounder tonight is from the Seto Inland Sea, it's the best I've had this season, and it's only available until the end of the month"
- Is offered once, not pushed: if the guest declines, accept it gracefully and move on
- Is honest: if something isn't at its best tonight, say so and point them toward the better option

That last point trips people up. Steering a guest away from a menu item feels counterintuitive, but it's exactly what builds trust. A guest who learns you redirected them away from an off night will trust every recommendation you make from that point on.

**The seasonal recommendation:**

The most natural recommendation in a Japanese context is the seasonal one: *"Tonight's ○○ is at peak season right now -- if you enjoy ○○, this is a genuinely good time to try it."* This carries no sales pressure because it's real, time-sensitive information. The guest understands they may not have this opportunity again soon.

**When to recommend and when not to:**

Not every interaction calls for a recommendation. A guest who has clearly decided what they want should have their order taken without interruption. Worth noting: a guest who is studying the menu with visible uncertainty, or who asks directly "what do you suggest?" -- that's your invitation. Respond thoughtfully. The standard is simple: offer your genuine opinion when it's invited or clearly useful. Don't fill every interaction with suggestions the guest didn't ask for.

## Key Phrases

**もしよろしければ、本日のおすすめをご紹介してもよろしいでしょうか？** *(Moshi yoroshikereba, honjitsu no o-susume wo go-shōkai shite mo yoroshii deshō ka?)*
"If I may, would you allow me to introduce today's recommendations?": a polite request to offer suggestions rather than delivering them unsolicited.

**こちらは今が旬の時期で、特に風味が豊かでございます。** *(Kochira wa ima ga shun no jiki de, toku ni fūmi ga yutaka de gozaimasu.)*
"This is at its seasonal peak right now: the flavour is particularly rich.": the seasonal recommendation, grounded in genuine reason.

**おっしゃる通りでございます。では、こちらはいかがでしょうか。** *(Ossharu tōri de gozaimasu. Dewa, kochira wa ikaga deshō ka.)*
"You are absolutely right. In that case, might I suggest this one?": accepting a guest's feedback on a recommendation and offering an alternative, without defensiveness.

## Quiz

**A guest has ordered two light fish courses and is looking at the dessert menu. They ask: "What do you recommend for dessert?" What is the most omotenashi-aligned response?**

- A. Recommend the most expensive dessert on the menu
- B. Recommend the most popular dessert, regardless of what the guest has eaten
- C. Recommend a dessert that complements the light, delicate nature of their meal: ideally the seasonal or in-house preparation: and explain why it suits what they have been eating
- D. Say "everything is delicious" and encourage them to choose

**Correct Answer:** Recommend a dessert that complements the light, delicate nature of their meal: ideally the seasonal or in-house preparation: and explain why it suits what they have been eating

**Explanation:** A good recommendation follows from what the guest has already experienced. After two light fish courses, a heavy, rich dessert may not be the right ending. A recommendation that considers the arc of the meal: and explains the reasoning: shows genuine knowledge and care. It is an act of hospitality, not sales.
