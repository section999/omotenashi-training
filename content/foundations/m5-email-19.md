A single incorrect phrase can undermine an otherwise well-written email. The five areas below highlight where errors most commonly occur. Use this checklist before sending any hotel email.

## The Pre-Send Checklist

Use this checklist before sending every hotel email.

**Subject**<br>
☐ Uses 【】 to indicate email type<br>
☐ Includes guest name and key detail (date or topic)<br>
☐ Purpose is clear before opening

**Opening**<br>
☐ Matches guest type (first-time / returning / VIP)<br>
☐ Matches email purpose (reservation / inquiry / apology / notice)<br>
☐ Uses correct tense (ありがとうございます / ありがとうございました)

**Body**<br>
☐ One topic per paragraph<br>
☐ Facts written using でございます<br>
☐ Requests use soft form: ○○いただけますと幸いでございます<br>
☐ No vague expressions (たぶん / かもしれない) when clarity is required

**Tone**<br>
☐ No excuses in apology emails<br>
☐ No excessive or unnatural keigo<br>
☐ No casual expressions (ね / よ / informal phrasing)<br>
☐ Register stays consistent from opening to closing

**Closing**<br>
☐ Closing phrase is always present; no abrupt endings<br>
☐ Matches email type to closing phrase

## Quiz

**A staff member drafts a reservation confirmation email. The subject line reads 「ご連絡」, and the body includes the sentence 「チェックインはたぶん15時頃になると思います」. Which two checklist sections does this email fail?**

- A. Subject and Body
- B. Opening and Tone
- C. Tone and Closing
- D. Body and Closing

**Correct Answer:** A

**Explanation:** The subject line 「ご連絡」fails the Subject checklist on two counts: it does not use 【】 to indicate the email type, and the purpose is not clear before opening. A correct subject would be 【ご予約確認】田中様｜7月15日チェックイン. The body sentence fails the Body checklist: たぶん and 思います are vague expressions explicitly prohibited when clarity is required. A guest asking about check-in time needs a definite answer such as 「チェックインは15時からでございます」. The Opening, Tone, and Closing sections are not affected by these two errors.
