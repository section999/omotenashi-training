title: Core Telephone Keigo: Acknowledgment, Wait, and Transfer
---

In high-pressure situations, complaints, rush hours, unexpected requests, there is no time to construct polished sentences from scratch. Fixed telephone expressions eliminate this risk. They allow staff to respond immediately, accurately, and professionally without hesitation.

This lesson covers acknowledgment, wait and hold, and transfer expressions: the three most frequent categories in telephone communications.

## Acknowledgment Expressions

<div class="keigo-card blue">
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>かしこまりました</code></span><br>
<em>kashikomarimashita</em>: Certainly (highest formality; used when receiving requests or instructions from a guest)<br><br>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>承知いたしました</code></span><br>
<em>shouchi itashimashita</em>: Understood (formal; used when receiving information the guest provides)<br><br>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>さようでございますか</code></span><br>
<em>sayou de gozaimasu ka</em>: Is that so? (formal empathetic response; shows you are listening closely)
</div>

## Wait and Hold Expressions

<div class="keigo-card blue">
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>少々お待ちくださいませ</code></span><br>
<em>shoushou o-machi kudasaimase</em>: Please wait a moment<br><br>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>確認いたします</code></span><br>
<em>kakunin itashimasu</em>: I will confirm (always precede a hold with this phrase)<br><br>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>大変お待たせいたしました</code></span><br>
<em>taihen o-matase itashimashita</em>: Thank you very much for waiting (use when returning after any wait)
</div>

## Transfer and Connection Expressions

<div class="keigo-card blue">
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>おつなぎいたします</code></span><br>
<em>o-tsunagi itashimasu</em>: I will connect you (frames transfer as coordination, not abandonment)<br><br>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>担当者にお繋ぎいたします</code></span><br>
<em>tantousha ni o-tsunagi itashimasu</em>: I will connect you to the person in charge<br><br>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>折り返しご連絡いたします</code></span><br>
<em>orikaeshi go-renraku itashimasu</em>: I will call you back
</div>

## Quiz

**Which phrase should always be used before placing a guest on hold?**

- A. 少々お待ちください
- B. 確認いたしますので、少々お待ちくださいませ
- C. ちょっと待ってください
- D. 少し確認します

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Before placing a guest on hold, staff must state what they are confirming (確認いたします) and use ので to connect it to the wait request. Adding くださいませ makes the request polite rather than instructional. Simply saying 少々お待ちください omits the reason, leaving the guest uncertain about why they are waiting. ちょっと待ってください is too casual for hotel service.
