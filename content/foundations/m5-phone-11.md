Late arrival notices are among the most operationally consequential schedule-based calls. Without accurate records, rooms may be released during night audit, creating a situation where a paying guest arrives to find no room available. This is an irreversible operational failure.

## Why Time Repetition Cannot Be Skipped

| Request Type | If Time Is Wrong | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Wake-up call | 6am logged as 7am | Guest misses flight or meeting |
| Taxi | 3pm logged as 2pm | Guest stranded or late |
| Late arrival | 11pm not logged | Room released; no-show recorded |

Unlike other service errors that can be corrected, time-based failures often cannot be undone. The guest's schedule has already been affected. This is why **repeating the time is not a formality; it is the most critical verification step in schedule-based calls**.

## Quiz

**Why must a late arrival time be logged immediately after the call?**

- A. To prepare the correct room temperature in advance
- B. Rooms without a logged late arrival may be released during night audit, leaving the guest with no room on arrival
- C. To notify housekeeping to prepare extra pillows
- D. So the front desk knows when to order the guest's breakfast

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Late arrival records prevent the night audit process from releasing rooms that appear unoccupied. If a late arrival is not logged, the room may be marked as available and given to another guest. The arriving guest then finds no room; an operational failure that cannot be corrected on the spot. Repeating and immediately logging the arrival time is the final safety step in this call type.
