title: Opening Lines by Guest Relationship
---

The opening line of a hotel email carries two messages simultaneously: what you are saying, and how well you know the person you are saying it to. Choosing the wrong opening signals that the hotel treats guests interchangeably. Choosing the right one communicates: "We know who you are."

## Three Relationship Levels

Hotel email openings fall into three relationship categories. Before writing any email, identify which category the guest belongs to.

<div class="keigo-card blue">
<span class="keigo-badge info">First-Time Guest</span> No prior relationship. New booking, first inquiry, or first contact. The opening must close the gap with warmth and genuine welcome.<br><br>
<span class="keigo-badge info">Repeat Guest</span> The guest has stayed before or has an ongoing relationship with the hotel. The opening must acknowledge this history explicitly.<br><br>
<span class="keigo-badge info">VIP / Corporate / Long-Term</span> The guest has elevated status, a long history, or a formal business relationship. The opening requires refined language and sometimes personalization.
</div>

## Opening for First-Time Guests

<div class="keigo-card blue">
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>この度は、ご予約いただき、誠にありがとうございます。</code></span><br>
Thank you very much for making a reservation on this occasion.<br><br>
<strong>Key phrase: この度は</strong><br>
この度は means "on this occasion" or "this time." It naturally implies that this is a new or significant event, making it the standard phrase for first-time interactions. It signals that the hotel recognizes this as the beginning of a relationship.<br><br>
<strong>Alternative (inquiry response):</strong><br>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>この度は、ご連絡いただき、誠にありがとうございます。</code></span><br>
Thank you very much for reaching out on this occasion.
</div>

## Opening for Repeat Guests

<div class="keigo-card blue">
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>いつもお世話になっております。</code></span><br>
Thank you for your continued support.<br><br>
<strong>Key phrase: いつもお世話になっております</strong><br>
いつも means "always" and assumes a repeated relationship. This phrase acknowledges loyalty, communicates recognition, and creates emotional continuity. It is the standard repeat-guest opening across all Japanese business correspondence.<br><br>
<strong>For a guest returning after a long gap:</strong><br>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>ご無沙汰しております。この度は、またご予約いただき、誠にありがとうございます。</code></span><br>
It has been a while. Thank you very much for booking with us again on this occasion.
</div>

## Opening for VIP or Long-Term Guests

<div class="keigo-card blue">
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>平素より格別のご愛顧を賜り、誠にありがとうございます。</code></span><br>
Thank you sincerely for your continued and generous patronage.<br><br>
<strong>Key phrases:</strong><br>
平素より (heizo yori), "always" with more formal weight than いつも<br>
格別のご愛顧 (kakubetsu no go-aiko), "generous patronage," elevated over ordinary repeat-guest language<br>
賜り (tamawari), humble receiving, more formal than いただき<br><br>
Use this opening for VIP guests, corporate accounts, or guests with long histories at luxury properties.
</div>

## The Critical Rule: Match Opening to Relationship

Using the wrong opening communicates either carelessness or system automation. Both are harmful to the guest relationship.

<span class="keigo-jp"><code>いつもお世話になっております。</code></span><br>
(sent to someone who has never stayed before)<br><br>
<span class="keigo-badge bad">Error</span> いつも assumes a history that does not exist. The guest feels seen as a number.

## Quiz

**A guest has stayed at the hotel twice before and is booking again. Which opening is correct?**

- A. 「この度は、ご予約いただき、誠にありがとうございます」
- B. 「はじめまして」
- C. 「いつもお世話になっております」
- D. 「ご無沙汰しております」

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** いつもお世話になっております is the standard opening for guests with an established relationship. It acknowledges their history with the hotel and communicates recognition and appreciation. Option A (この度は) is for first-time guests. Option B (はじめまして) is for the very first introduction and would be strange in an email to a known guest. Option D (ご無沙汰しております) implies a long gap in communication, which is not the case here.
