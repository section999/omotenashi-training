## Mistake 1: Mixing Casual and Formal Language

<div class="keigo-pair">
<div class="bad-col">
<span class="keigo-pair-label">Wrong</span>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>少々待ってください</code></span>
</div>
<div class="good-col">
<span class="keigo-pair-label">Better</span>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>少々お待ちくださいませ</code></span>
</div>
</div>

Part formal, part casual. This creates inconsistent tone.

## Mistake 2: Forgetting Guest Elevation

<div class="keigo-pair">
<div class="bad-col">
<span class="keigo-pair-label">Wrong</span>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>お客様は食べましたか？</code></span>
</div>
<div class="good-col">
<span class="keigo-pair-label">Better</span>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>お客様は召し上がりましたか？</code></span>
</div>
</div>

Uses plain verb 食べる for guest action. Always elevate the guest with 尊敬語.

## Mistake 3: Overusing Difficult Keigo

<div class="keigo-pair">
<div class="bad-col">
<span class="keigo-pair-label">Wrong</span>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>お伺いさせていただきます</code></span>
</div>
<div class="good-col">
<span class="keigo-pair-label">Better</span>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>お伺いします</code></span>
</div>
</div>

Unnatural double keigo, sounds excessive. Professional keigo should sound controlled and natural.

## Mistake 4: Translating Directly from English

<div class="keigo-pair">
<div class="bad-col">
<span class="keigo-pair-label">Wrong</span>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>チェックインしたいです</code></span>
</div>
<div class="good-col">
<span class="keigo-pair-label">Better</span>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>チェックインをお願いいたします</code></span>
</div>
</div>

"Check-in want to do" sounds too self-centered in Japanese hospitality settings. Japanese hotel language focuses on smoother interpersonal balance.

---

## Quiz

**What is the problem with 「お伺いさせていただきます」？**

- A. It is grammatically correct
- B. It is unnatural double keigo that sounds excessive
- C. It is too casual
- D. It is the best choice for hotels

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** お伺いさせていただきます is an example of double keigo (overly humble). It sounds unnatural and excessive. Simple お伺いします is more controlled and professional.
