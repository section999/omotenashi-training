title: Social Media and Guest Privacy
---

A single post by a guest, whether glowing or critical, can reach thousands of people before the property is even aware of it. A genuinely warm experience shared publicly provides visibility that no advertising budget can replicate. A poorly handled public complaint can do lasting damage. Social media also creates privacy risks that deserve careful attention.

## The Guest's Right to Privacy

Everything that applies to discretion in person applies online as well, with higher stakes because scale is instant. A staff member who photographs a guest and shares it privately has still committed a serious breach of trust. Staff policies on personal device use in guest areas exist to protect guests, who have the right to move through the property without their presence appearing on someone else's phone.

## When Guests Post About the Property

**Positive posts**: If a guest tags the property, a warm and brief reply from the official account is appropriate. It shows the guest their experience was noticed and demonstrates to other readers that the property pays attention. Always respond in the same language the guest used.

**Negative posts**: A public complaint requires a public acknowledgement, followed by an invitation to take the conversation private: "We are sorry to hear this was not the experience we intended. Please message us directly so we can address this properly." The resolution happens off the public thread. The public acknowledgement matters because other people are watching.

## The Property's Own Social Media

Sharing a guest photograph on the property's account requires explicit written permission first. Tagging a guest without consent, even in a positive context, is a privacy breach. Keep a record of all permissions given.

## Staff Personal Accounts

Staff should not post about guests, share images taken in guest areas during working hours, or publish anything that reveals how the operation runs. "Just my close friends" is not a reliable safeguard. A clear social media policy communicated at induction and reinforced regularly is the only effective protection.

## Quiz

**A guest posts a photo on Instagram of a staff member helping them with luggage at check-in, captioning it "Best service ever at [property name]!" and tagging the property. The staff member is clearly identifiable in the photo and did not give consent to be photographed. The post is receiving many positive reactions and comments. What is the most appropriate response?**

- A. Repost the photo on the property's official account to show appreciation for the guest's kind words and to encourage similar positive posts
- B. Leave the post unacknowledged publicly; engaging with it could draw more attention to the privacy concern
- C. Reply publicly thanking the guest for the warm words, then privately message the guest to politely explain the property's staff privacy policy and request that the photo be removed or the staff member's face blurred
- D. Untag the property from the post and ask the staff member to contact the guest directly to request removal

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** This scenario presents a genuine tension between two valid principles: the marketing and relationship value of a positive guest post, and the staff member's right to privacy and consent. Option C resolves the dilemma most effectively: the public reply acknowledges and thanks the guest (maintaining the positive relationship and showing future guests the property is attentive), while the private message handles the consent issue discreetly without embarrassing the guest publicly. Option A violates staff privacy by amplifying the breach. Option B avoids the problem entirely, leaving the photo up without consent. Option D inappropriately shifts the burden of a policy matter onto an individual staff member, which is unprofessional.
