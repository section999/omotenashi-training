title: Social Media and Guest Privacy
---

Social media has fundamentally changed the relationship between guests, properties, and the public. A guest's single post — positive or negative — can reach thousands of people instantly. A staff member's careless photograph can become a privacy violation before the shift ends. Managing the digital presence of a hospitality property requires both strategy and discretion.

**The guest's right to privacy in the digital age:**

Every principle of discretion that applies in person applies online — amplified. A photograph of a well-known guest taken by a staff member and shared, even privately, is a serious breach. An overheard conversation about a guest's stay posted on social media is a crisis. The test remains: if the guest could see this, would they feel safe and respected?

Staff policies on personal device use in guest areas exist for this reason. They are not about distrust of staff — they are about protecting guests from the unintended consequences of a world where everything is one tap from being public.

**When guests post about the property:**

Positive posts: If a guest tags the property in a positive post, a warm, brief response from the property's official account is appropriate — it acknowledges the guest and the public sees a property that is engaged and grateful. Always respond in the same language the guest used.

Negative posts: A public complaint on social media should be acknowledged publicly with empathy and a private follow-up: *"We are sorry to hear this was not the experience we intended. Please message us directly so we can address this properly."* Move the resolution out of the public thread.

**The property's own social media:**

Guest photographs shared on the property's account require the guest's explicit permission. Tagging a guest without their consent — even in a positive context — is a privacy violation. Obtain permission clearly and document it.

**Staff personal accounts:**

Staff should not post about guests, post from guest areas during working hours, or post content that reveals confidential operational information. A clear social media policy, communicated at induction, is essential.

## Quiz

**A guest posts a negative comment about the property on social media, tagging the property's account. What is the correct first response?**

- A. Delete the comment if possible to prevent others from seeing it
- B. Reply publicly with a full explanation of why the guest's complaint is incorrect
- C. Acknowledge the comment publicly with empathy and invite the guest to contact the property privately to resolve it
- D. Ignore it — responding publicly gives the complaint more visibility

**Correct Answer:** Acknowledge the comment publicly with empathy and invite the guest to contact the property privately to resolve it

**Explanation:** A public acknowledgement shows other readers that the property is responsive and caring. Moving the resolution to a private channel allows the issue to be addressed properly without further public escalation. Ignoring, deleting, or arguing publicly all damage the property's reputation more than the original complaint.
