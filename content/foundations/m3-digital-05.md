title: Social Media and Guest Privacy
---

A single post by a guest, whether glowing or critical, can reach thousands of people before you even know it exists. That reality cuts both ways: a genuinely warm experience shared publicly is free advertising that no budget can replicate, and a poorly handled complaint that plays out in public can do lasting damage. Social media also creates privacy risks that most staff underestimate until something goes wrong.

**The guest's right to privacy in the digital age:**

Everything you know about discretion in person applies online too, but the stakes are higher because scale is instant. A staff member who photographs a well-known guest and shares it privately with friends has still committed a serious breach. There is no version of that where the guest would feel safe and respected if they found out. Staff policies on personal device use in guest areas exist not because management distrusts its team, but because guests deserve to move through the property without worrying that their presence will end up on someone's phone.

**When guests post about the property:**

Positive posts: If a guest tags the property, a warm and brief reply from the official account is exactly right. It shows the guest their experience was noticed and shows everyone else that the property pays attention. Always respond in the same language the guest used.

Negative posts: A public complaint needs a public acknowledgement, followed by an invitation to take it private: *"We are sorry to hear this was not the experience we intended. Please message us directly so we can address this properly."* The resolution happens off the public thread. Students often want to skip the public response and go straight to a private message, but the public acknowledgement matters because other people are watching.

**The property's own social media:**

Sharing a guest photograph on the property's account requires explicit permission first. Tagging a guest without consent, even in a context where they look wonderful, is a privacy breach. Get the permission in writing and keep a record of it.

**Staff personal accounts:**

Staff should not post about guests, share images taken in guest areas during working hours, or put out anything that reveals how the operation runs. One more thing to be clear about: "just my close friends" is not a meaningful protection. A clear social media policy communicated at induction, and reinforced regularly, is the only reliable safeguard.

## Key Phrases

**ご不便をおかけして誠に申し訳ございません。** *(Go-fuben o okake shite makoto ni mōshiwake gozaimasen.)*
"We sincerely apologise for the inconvenience.": the opening of a public social media response to a negative post; brief, genuine, and visible to all who read the thread

**詳細につきましては、直接ご連絡いただけますでしょうか。** *(Shōsai ni tsukimashite wa, chokusetsu go-renraku itadakemasu deshō ka.)*
"Could you contact us directly so we can address this properly?": the phrase that moves a public dispute to a private channel; shows responsiveness while taking the resolution out of public view

**お客様のプライバシーを最優先に考えております。** *(O-kyakusama no puraibashī o saiyūsen ni kangaete orimasu.)*
"We place the highest priority on our guests' privacy.": used when a guest enquires about photo consent or privacy; communicates the property's commitment clearly and without defensive language

## Quiz

**A guest posts a negative comment about the property on social media, tagging the property's account. What is the correct first response?**

- A. Delete the comment if possible to prevent others from seeing it
- B. Reply publicly with a full explanation of why the guest's complaint is incorrect
- C. Acknowledge the comment publicly with empathy and invite the guest to contact the property privately to resolve it
- D. Ignore it: responding publicly gives the complaint more visibility

**Correct Answer:** Acknowledge the comment publicly with empathy and invite the guest to contact the property privately to resolve it

**Explanation:** A public acknowledgement shows other readers that the property is responsive and caring. Moving the resolution to a private channel allows the issue to be addressed properly without further public escalation. Ignoring, deleting, or arguing publicly all damage the property's reputation more than the original complaint.
