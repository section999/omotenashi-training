title: 尊敬語: The Core Rule
---

尊敬語 (sonkeigo) is the honorific system used to elevate the guest's actions. Whenever the guest is the subject of a sentence, the verb must take its sonkeigo form. This is not optional in hotel service; it is the grammatical expression of the hospitality relationship.

## The Core Decision

Before selecting any verb form in hotel speech, ask one question: **who is performing this action?**

| Subject | Keigo Type | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Guest is acting | <span>尊敬語</span> | Elevates the guest's action |
| Staff is acting | <span>謙譲語</span> | Lowers the staff's position |
| General statement | <span>丁寧語</span> | Maintains professional tone |

This single decision drives every verb choice in hotel keigo.

## Why the Rule Matters

Using a plain verb when the guest is the subject sounds fine in everyday conversation. In hotel service, it is a register error. The difference is not rudeness; it is the absence of professional care.

The correction is not about using fancier words. It reflects whether the staff has internalized that the guest's actions are categorically different from their own.

## Quiz

**Which of the following correctly identifies when 尊敬語 is required?**

- A. When the staff member is explaining hotel rules
- B. When the guest is performing the action
- C. When the sentence contains a Japanese-origin noun
- D. When speaking to a colleague about a guest

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** 尊敬語 is required whenever the guest is the subject performing the action. It is not triggered by the type of noun, the presence of a colleague, or general explanations. The core rule is simple: guest as actor equals 尊敬語. This decision comes before any other grammatical choice in hotel speech.
