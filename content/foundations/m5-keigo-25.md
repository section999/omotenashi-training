title: Edge Cases: Third Parties and Guest Families
---

Standard keigo applies clearly when the subject is the guest or the staff. Two common edge cases require additional judgment: third parties (colleagues, other staff) and the guest's family members.

## Edge Case 1: Third Parties and Colleagues

When a colleague is the subject, neither sonkeigo nor kenjogo is appropriate. Sonkeigo would elevate the colleague above the guest; kenjogo implies the colleague is humbly serving the guest directly. The correct approach is to use おります, the humble existence form, when referring to a colleague's presence.

<div class="keigo-pair">
<div class="bad-col">
<span class="keigo-pair-label">Wrong</span>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>担当者がいらっしゃいます。</code></span><br>
<em>tantousha ga irasshaimasu.</em><br>
The person in charge is here.<br><br>
<span class="keigo-badge bad">Wrong</span> いらっしゃいます is 尊敬語; applying it to a colleague elevates them above the guest.
</div>
<div class="good-col">
<span class="keigo-pair-label">Correct</span>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>担当者がおります。</code></span><br>
<em>tantousha ga orimasu.</em><br>
The person in charge is here.<br><br>
<span class="keigo-badge good">Correct</span> おります is 謙譲語 for いる; it keeps the colleague in an appropriately lower position than the guest.
</div>
</div>

For a neutral third party such as another hotel, use standard polite language:

<div class="keigo-card blue">
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>近隣のホテルをご案内することができます。</code></span><br>
<em>kinrin no hoteru o go-annai suru koto ga dekimasu.</em><br>
I can provide information about nearby hotels.
</div>

## Edge Case 2: The Guest's Family Members

When referring to the guest's family, use 尊敬語 because they are guests too; their connection to the guest extends the honorific context to them.

<div class="keigo-card blue">
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>ご家族様もいらっしゃいますか。</code></span><br>
<em>go-kazoku-sama mo irasshaimasu ka.</em><br>
Will your family be joining you?<br><br>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>お子様はお召し上がりになりますか。</code></span><br>
<em>o-kosama wa o-meshiagari ni narimasu ka.</em><br>
Will your children be eating?
</div>

Both ご家族様 and お子様 use honorific prefixes because they refer to people connected to the guest, and the 尊敬語 context extends to them.

## Quiz

**A colleague is about to help a guest. How does the front desk staff explain this to the guest?**

- A. 担当者がいらっしゃいます。
- B. 担当者が参ります。
- C. 担当者がおります。ご案内いたします。
- D. 担当者がございます。

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** 担当者がおります correctly uses おります (謙譲語 for いる) for the colleague, keeping them in a position lower than the guest. ご案内いたします then uses the humble form for the action of guiding. Option A uses いらっしゃいます (尊敬語) for the colleague, incorrectly elevating them. Option B uses 参ります (謙譲語 for going), which works for the staff's movement but is better reserved for the staff's own actions, not describing a colleague as a static presence. Option D uses ございます for a person, which is only appropriate for objects and facts.
