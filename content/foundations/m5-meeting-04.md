---

Japanese hotel handover writing follows strict rules.

## Rule 1: Absolute Objectivity

<div class="keigo-pair">
<div class="bad-col">
<span class="keigo-pair-label">Wrong</span>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>ちょっとエアコンが変な感じです</code></span>
<br><br>
<span class="keigo-badge bad">Subjective</span> This is subjective and unclear.
</div>
<div class="good-col">
<span class="keigo-pair-label">Correct</span>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>空調不良あり</code></span>
<br><br>
<span class="keigo-badge good">Factual</span> Clear and objective.
</div>
</div>

Or more completely:

<div class="keigo-card green">
<span class="keigo-card-title">Good Example</span>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>305号室：空調不良。修理手配済み。</code></span>
</div>

## Rule 2: No Softening Words

<div class="keigo-card red">
<span class="keigo-card-title">Forbidden Words</span>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>ちょっと</code></span>, <span class="keigo-jp"><code>たぶん</code></span>, <span class="keigo-jp"><code>〜と思います</code></span>, <span class="keigo-jp"><code>なんか</code></span>
<br><br>
These are not allowed in operational notes.
</div>

## Rule 3: Use "Status First" Structure

<div class="keigo-card blue">
<span class="keigo-card-title">Format</span>
<strong>[Room / Guest] + [Issue] + [Status]</strong>
<br><br>
<span class="keigo-badge info">Example</span> <span class="keigo-jp"><code>210号室：騒音クレームあり。夜間対応済み。</code></span>
</div>

## Rule 4: No Explanation Culture

<div class="keigo-pair">
<div class="bad-col">
<span class="keigo-pair-label">Bad</span>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>おそらく機械が少し古いので壊れたと思います</code></span>
<br><br>
<span class="keigo-badge bad">Wordy</span> Too much reasoning.
</div>
<div class="good-col">
<span class="keigo-pair-label">Good</span>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>設備不良。修理依頼済み。</code></span>
<br><br>
<span class="keigo-badge good">Concise</span> Just facts.
</div>
</div>

## Quiz

**Which word is NOT allowed in professional hotel handover writing?**

- A. 対応済み
- B. たぶん
- C. 修理手配済み
- D. 要対応

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** たぶん (maybe) is a softening word that creates ambiguity. Handover notes must be factual and unambiguous. Other options are all acceptable operational terms.
