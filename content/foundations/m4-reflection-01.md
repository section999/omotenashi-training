title: Hansei — The Practice of Self-Reflection
---

In Japanese professional culture, *hansei* (反省) — self-reflection — is not an occasional exercise. It is a discipline, practised regularly and deliberately, as an integral part of professional life. The character 反 means "to turn back" or "to reconsider," and 省 means "to examine" or "to inspect." Together, they describe the act of turning one's attention inward — looking carefully and honestly at one's own actions, decisions, and conduct — with the aim of understanding what happened and how to improve.

Hansei is not self-criticism. It is not dwelling on failure, assigning blame to oneself, or generating guilt. It is a systematic, calm, and honest examination of reality, conducted with the intention of growth. In the context of omotenashi, hansei is the practice that allows a hospitality professional to improve continuously — not just when formal training is provided, but through the daily rhythm of service itself.

### The Difference Between Hansei and Regret

Regret is passive and backward-looking. It circles the same moment without moving forward. Hansei is active and forward-looking. It asks not just "what went wrong?" but "what did I not notice? What assumption did I make that was incorrect? What would I do differently, and why?" These are generative questions — they produce insight that can be applied in the next interaction.

### How to Practise Hansei

Hansei does not require a formal setting or a long block of time. It can be practised in the natural pauses of a shift: a quiet moment before the next guest arrives, the walk between floors, the few minutes at the end of a service.

A simple hansei practice for hospitality professionals:
1. **Identify a moment** — a specific interaction, decision, or response
2. **Describe what happened** — factually, without judgement
3. **Ask what you noticed** — and what you may have missed
4. **Ask what you would do differently** — and why
5. **Name one thing to try differently next time**

The final step is the most important: hansei without a specific intention to act differently is incomplete. The purpose of looking back is to move forward more skillfully.

## Key Phrases

**反省 (hansei)** — self-reflection; the deliberate, honest examination of one's own conduct and decisions for the purpose of growth; a central practice of Japanese professional and personal life

**次はもっとうまくできると思います (tsugi wa motto umaku dekiru to omoimasu)** *(Tsugi wa motto umaku dekiru to omoimasu.)*
"I believe I can do better next time." — the natural conclusion of a genuine hansei practice; a commitment forward, not a dwelling backward

## Quiz

**What distinguishes hansei (反省) from simple regret?**

- A. Hansei focuses on assigning blame; regret focuses on personal feelings
- B. Hansei is a structured, forward-looking reflection aimed at improvement; regret circles the same moment without generating change
- C. Hansei is only practised by senior staff; regret is for everyone
- D. There is no meaningful difference — both are forms of self-criticism

**Correct Answer:** Hansei is a structured, forward-looking reflection aimed at improvement; regret circles the same moment without generating change

**Explanation:** Hansei is generative — it produces insight and specific intention to act differently. It is not self-criticism or dwelling; it is the disciplined turning of attention toward one's own practice in the service of continuous growth. The difference is orientation: backward for understanding, forward for action.
