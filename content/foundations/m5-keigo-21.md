The front desk requires the widest keigo range of any hotel department. Staff must handle arrivals, identity confirmations, reservation management, problem-solving, and farewells; all in rapid succession and often under time pressure.

## Core Front Desk Phrases

<div class="keigo-card yellow">
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>いらっしゃいませ。</code></span><br>
<em>irasshaimase.</em><br>
Welcome.<br><br>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>ご予約のお名前をお聞かせいただけますでしょうか。</code></span><br>
<em>go-yoyaku no o-namae o o-kikase itadakemasu deshou ka.</em><br>
Could you tell me the name on your reservation?<br><br>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>○○様でいらっしゃいますね。</code></span><br>
<em>○○-sama de irasshaimasu ne.</em><br>
You are Mr./Ms. ○○, correct?<br><br>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>こちらがお部屋の鍵でございます。</code></span><br>
<em>kochira ga o-heya no kagi de gozaimasu.</em><br>
Here is your room key.<br><br>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>何かございましたらお申し付けくださいませ。</code></span><br>
<em>nani ka gozaimashitara o-moushitsuke kudasaimase.</em><br>
Please let us know if you need anything.<br><br>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>またのお越しをお待ちしております。</code></span><br>
<em>mata no o-koshi o o-machi shite orimasu.</em><br>
We look forward to your next visit.
</div>

## Keigo Elements in Each Phrase

| Phrase | Keigo in Use |
|---|---|
| <span>いらっしゃいませ</span> | 尊敬語: welcomes the guest's arrival |
| <span>ご予約のお名前</span> | ご (漢語 prefix) + お (和語 prefix) |
| <span>お聞かせいただけますでしょうか</span> | Humble receiving + 丁寧語 question |
| <span>でいらっしゃいますね</span> | 尊敬語: confirms guest identity |
| <span>でございます</span> | 丁寧語: refined statement |
| <span>お申し付けくださいませ</span> | Polished instruction to the guest |
| <span>お待ちしております</span> | 謙譲語: humble waiting |

## Quiz

**A guest is leaving the hotel. Which farewell phrase is correct?**

- A. またきてください。
- B. さようなら。
- C. またのお越しをお待ちしております。
- D. じゃあ、また。

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** またのお越しをお待ちしております is the standard hotel farewell. お越し is the 尊敬語 prefix form for the guest's coming, and お待ちしております is the humble form of waiting. Together they communicate: "We humbly await your next honored visit." Options A, B, and D are too casual for hotel service; さようなら in particular sounds too final and is not used in hotels where the goal is to communicate that the guest is always welcome to return.
