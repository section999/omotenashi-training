The best way to learn handover writing is by comparing real mistakes with correct versions. Each case below shows a common error and how a professional rewrites it.

## Case 1: AC Failure (Critical Room Issue)

A guest reported that their air conditioning is not working. The bad version sounds like a casual conversation. The good version follows the standard format.

<div class="keigo-pair">
<div class="bad-col">
<span class="keigo-pair-label">Bad</span>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>エアコンがちょっとおかしい感じで、明日修理する予定です</code></span>
<br><br>
<span class="keigo-badge bad">Emotional</span> Uses ちょっと and 感じ, which are vague.<br>
<span class="keigo-badge bad">Unclear</span> No room number or specific problem.<br>
<span class="keigo-badge bad">No ownership</span> Who arranged the repair?
</div>
<div class="good-col">
<span class="keigo-pair-label">Good</span>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>305号室：空調不良。修理手配済み（明日対応予定）</code></span>
<br><br>
<span class="keigo-badge good">Room + Issue + Status</span> Follows the standard format.<br>
<span class="keigo-badge good">Specific</span> Room 305, AC issue, repair arranged.<br>
<span class="keigo-badge good">Timeline</span> Includes when the repair will happen.
</div>
</div>

## Case 2: Guest Complaint (Noise at Night)

A guest complained about noise. The bad version includes the staff's personal feelings. The good version states the situation without emotion.

<div class="keigo-pair">
<div class="bad-col">
<span class="keigo-pair-label">Bad</span>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>お客様がうるさいと言っていて少し困っている感じです</code></span>
<br><br>
<span class="keigo-badge bad">Subjective</span> The staff's feeling of being "troubled" is irrelevant. Handover notes do not need emotions.
</div>
<div class="good-col">
<span class="keigo-pair-label">Good</span>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>210号室：騒音クレームあり。夜間スタッフ対応済み。</code></span>
<br><br>
<span class="keigo-badge good">Objective</span> Room number, issue, and action taken. The next shift knows exactly what happened.
</div>
</div>

## Case 3: Extra Request

A guest asked for extra towels. The bad version reports the request like a story. The good version records it as a completed action.

<div class="keigo-pair">
<div class="bad-col">
<span class="keigo-pair-label">Bad</span>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>タオル欲しいと言われたので渡しました</code></span>
<br><br>
<span class="keigo-badge bad">Conversational</span> Written like spoken Japanese, not a handover note. Uses ので to explain cause and effect, which is unnecessary.
</div>
<div class="good-col">
<span class="keigo-pair-label">Good</span>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>タオル追加依頼あり。対応済み。</code></span>
<br><br>
<span class="keigo-badge good">Concise</span> States the request and confirms it was handled. No extra words.
</div>
</div>

## Case 4: Pending Request

A guest wants to extend their stay but the staff has not confirmed availability yet. This is an incomplete task that the next shift must follow up on.

<div class="keigo-pair">
<div class="bad-col">
<span class="keigo-pair-label">Bad</span>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>延泊したいって言ってました。まだ確認中です</code></span>
<br><br>
<span class="keigo-badge bad">Vague</span> Uses って quoting style (casual). Says 確認中 but does not say who is responsible or what is being checked.
</div>
<div class="good-col">
<span class="keigo-pair-label">Good</span>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>延泊希望あり。フロント確認待ち。</code></span>
<br><br>
<span class="keigo-badge good">Actionable</span> Clearly states what the guest wants and where the task is blocked (waiting for front desk confirmation). The next shift knows exactly what to do.
</div>
</div>

## Quiz

**Which is the correct handover format for a noise complaint?**

- A. ちょっと騒音問題がありました
- B. たぶんうるさかったみたいです
- C. お客様がうるさいと言っていました
- D. 210号室：騒音クレームあり。夜間スタッフ対応済み。

**Correct Answer:** D

**Explanation:** The correct format uses [Room] + [Issue] + [Status] structure. B is factual and actionable. A, C, and D are subjective, emotional, or unclear.
