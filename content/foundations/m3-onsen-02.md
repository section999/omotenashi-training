title: Onsen Etiquette, The Rules of the Bath
---

The communal onsen operates according to a set of customs that are not merely polite suggestions. They are the shared code that makes the experience safe, comfortable, and meaningful for everyone present. Knowing these rules in detail, including the reasoning behind them, allows staff to explain them clearly to guests who are nervous, unfamiliar, or uncertain.

## Before Entering the Bath

1. **Remove all clothing and leave it in the changing room.** Onsen are entered without swimwear in most traditional settings.
2. **Wash thoroughly at the shower station (カラン, *karan*).** Every bather must wash and rinse completely before entering the communal bath. This is both a hygiene requirement and a deeply held cultural expectation. Entering without washing is a serious breach.
3. **Rinse the shower station after use.** Leave the space ready for the next person.

## In the Bath

- **Enter slowly and quietly.** Onsen are places of calm. Splashing, loud conversation, and running are inappropriate.
- **Small towel handling.** The small modesty towel (*tenugui*) does not go into the bath. It may be placed folded on the head or left at the bath's edge.
- **No swimming or vigorous movement.** The bath is for soaking, not exercise.
- **Time your soak.** Onsen water is hot, typically 40 to 44°C. First-time bathers should soak in shorter intervals (10 to 15 minutes) and rest between sessions.
- **Hair must not touch the water.** Long hair should be tied up or pinned.

## After Bathing

- Rinse briefly in the shower before returning to the changing room.
- Pat dry before leaving the wet area to avoid slipping and to keep the changing room floor dry.
- Do not use a hair dryer in the wet area.

## Explaining the Rules to Guests

Guests often feel anxious about getting something wrong in an unfamiliar setting. When explaining these rules, frame them as part of the experience rather than as a list of things to avoid. The goal is to make guests feel prepared and confident.

## Key Phrases

**カラン (karan)**
*Karan.*
"Shower station."

The individual shower station at an onsen facility, used for washing thoroughly before entering the communal bath. Using the karan before entering is mandatory, not optional.

**ご入浴前に、シャワーでお体をよくお流しください。**
*Go-nyūyoku mae ni, shawā de o-karada o yoku o-nagashi kudasai.*
"Please wash thoroughly at the shower station before entering the bath."

The essential instruction for all first-time onsen guests. Delivered warmly at the changing room entrance or during the guest orientation.

**小タオルはお湯の中にお入れにならないようお願いいたします。**
*Ko-taoru wa o-yu no naka ni o-ire ni naranai yō o-negai itashimasu.*
"Please keep the small towel out of the water."

A key etiquette point that many first-time visitors overlook. Can be explained gently alongside the towel's permitted uses: folded on the head or left at the bath edge.

## Quiz

**What must every bather do before entering the communal onsen bath?**

- A. Soak feet in a footbath for five minutes
- B. Wash and rinse thoroughly at the shower station
- C. Request a towel from the attendant
- D. Sign a hygiene declaration form

**Correct Answer:** Wash and rinse thoroughly at the shower station

**Explanation:** Pre-bath washing at the karan station is non-negotiable: it protects the shared water and is a deeply held social expectation that every other bather depends on. Skipping this step is one of the most serious breaches of onsen etiquette and will be noticed. The other options describe things that are either not required at all or specific to particular facilities.
