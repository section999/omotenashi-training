title: Messaging Apps and Digital Guest Communication
---

Guests increasingly prefer to contact properties through LINE, WhatsApp, WeChat, or the messaging function on booking platforms rather than calling or writing a formal email. This shift benefits service: it lowers the barrier for guests to ask questions, flag small problems early, and stay in touch. The risk is that the informal nature of messaging apps can lead staff to drop the professional register they would maintain in other channels.

## Maintaining the Professional Register

Messaging apps feel casual because most people use them that way. A guest asking about check-in time on WhatsApp still deserves the same clarity and warmth as a guest at the front desk. The channel is different; the standard is not.

Avoid the following in guest messages:

<div class="keigo-card blue">
• Abbreviations (*"Sry for wait"*, *"thx for ur msg"*)<br>• Emoji in formal contexts (a brief, appropriate emoji can warm a personal conversation, but use judgment)<br>• One-word replies (*"OK"*, *"Sure"*) without context or warmth
</div>


## Response Speed

Messaging platforms create an expectation of faster replies than email, often within minutes during business hours. This means the channel must be properly staffed, or a clear auto-response should be set up telling guests exactly when they can expect a reply. Silence on a messaging platform reads as neglect in a way that a delayed email does not.

## Accuracy and Confirmation

Because messaging moves quickly, errors get through more easily. Before sending any message that contains a booking detail, a price, a room type, or a time, read it back. Sending the wrong check-in time is genuinely difficult to recover from once the guest has made plans around it.

## When to Move to Email

Complaints, refund discussions, contract terms, and anything that may need to be referenced later should be moved to email. A messaging thread is not the right place to resolve a significant service failure or agree to financial matters. If a guest raises a serious complaint through messaging, the first step is to acknowledge it warmly and invite them to continue the conversation by email or phone.

## Quiz

**A guest messages the property on WhatsApp asking to confirm their check-in time. The correct reply is:**

- A. "3pm 👍"
- B. "Hi! Yes 3pm is fine, see u then!"
- C. "Thank you for your message. Your check-in time is 3:00 p.m. We look forward to welcoming you. Please let us know if there is anything we can arrange in advance."
- D. "Check-in is at 3PM. Please bring your reservation confirmation."

**Correct Answer:** "Thank you for your message. Your check-in time is 3:00 p.m. We look forward to welcoming you. Please let us know if there is anything we can arrange in advance."

**Explanation:** A messaging app doesn't lower the service standard; it just changes the medium. The correct reply confirms the information clearly, uses full sentences, and opens the door for the guest to ask for more, rather than closing the exchange with a terse confirmation. The other options are either too casual, too clipped, or miss the warmth that turns a basic answer into a small moment of hospitality.
