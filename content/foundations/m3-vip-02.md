title: Discretion, The Art of Silence
---

Discretion is the most important quality in VIP service. It is not a skill but a disposition: the understanding that what you know about a guest is held in trust, and that trust is absolute.

## What Discretion Means in Practice

• Never confirm or deny a VIP guest's presence to anyone, media, other guests, callers, or people claiming to be family or colleagues. The standard response to any such enquiry is: *"I'm afraid I'm not able to share information about our guests."*
• Never discuss guests with colleagues outside of operational necessity. The detail that a notable guest ordered room service at 2 a.m. is not a story to share, not even in the staff room.
• Never photograph, approach for personal reasons, or request autographs. A VIP guest who is recognised by a staff member in a way that draws attention has been let down by that property.
• Manage the environment. If a VIP guest is dining, stay alert to other guests who may recognise them. If needed, manage sightlines, seat the VIP in a more private position, or gently redirect the attention of others.


## The Standard to Apply

True discretion is not simply avoiding obvious violations. It is behaving as though the guest's information exists for no purpose other than serving them.

A useful test: if this guest were listening to every internal conversation about them, would they feel secure? That is the standard to meet.

## Internal Language

Use room numbers rather than personal names in radio communications. *"Can housekeeping check room 812?"* is preferable to *"Can housekeeping go to the senator's suite?"* One mentions a room. The other announces a guest. The difference matters.

## Quiz

**A VIP guest is dining in the restaurant when another guest approaches a staff member and says excitedly: "I think that is [famous person] at table 7! I am a huge fanI just want to say hello briefly." The other guest is a loyal returning customer. What is the most appropriate response?**

- A. Confirm quietly that it is indeed the VIP guest and ask the other guest to be very discreet if they approach
- B. Say warmly: "I am afraid I am not able to share information about our guests." Then redirect: "Would you like me to seat you in a different area where you might feel more comfortable?"
- C. Tell the other guest you cannot confirm anything, but suggest they could approach the table politely and introduce themselves
- D. Deny that the VIP guest is staying at the property to protect their privacy, since the other guest is persistent

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** This scenario requires weighing the absolute discretion principle (never confirm or deny a guest's presence) against the need to manage the environment warmly for all guests, including a loyal returning customer. Option B correctly applies the neither-confirm-nor-deny standard while proactively managing the situation by offering to reseat the other guestprotecting the VIP's privacy and the other guest's feelings simultaneously. Option A breaches discretion by confirming the VIP's presence. Option C directs the guest toward the VIP, creating an intrusion that the VIP cannot escape without causing a scene. Option D uses a flat denial, which the lesson identifies as inappropriate if untrue, and leaves the other guest without a graceful resolution.
