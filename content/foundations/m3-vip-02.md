**Discretion** is the most important quality in VIP service, and the hardest to train, because it is not a skill but a disposition. It is the understanding that what you know about a guest is held in trust, and that trust is absolute.

**What discretion means in practice:**

- **Never confirm or deny a VIP guest's presence** to anyone: media, other guests, callers, even people claiming to be family or colleagues. The standard response to any such enquiry is: *"I'm afraid I'm not able to share information about our guests."*
- **Never discuss guests with colleagues outside of operational necessity.** The detail that a famous guest ordered room service at 2 a.m. is not a story to share. Not even in the staff room. Students are sometimes surprised how clear this line needs to be, but it does. The gossip feels harmless. It isn't.
- **Never photograph, approach for personal reasons, or request autographs.** A VIP guest who is recognised by a staff member in a way that draws attention has been let down by that property.
- **Manage the environment**: If a VIP guest is dining, stay alert to other guests who may recognise them. If needed, manage sightlines, seat the VIP in a more private position, or gently redirect the attention of other guests.

**The test of discretion:**

True discretion is not simply avoiding obvious violations. It is behaving as though the guest's information does not exist for any purpose other than serving them. Here is the question to ask yourself: if this guest were listening to every internal conversation about them, would they feel secure? That is the standard.

**Internal language:**

In practice, use codenames or room numbers rather than personal names in radio communications. *"Can housekeeping check room 812?"* is preferable to *"Can housekeeping go to the senator's suite?"* One mentions a room. The other announces a guest. The difference matters.

## Key Phrases

**お客様に関する情報はお答えいたしかねます。** *(O-kyakusama ni kan suru jōhō wa o-kotae itashikane masu.)*
"I'm afraid I'm unable to share information about our guests.": the standard refusal for any enquiry about a guest's presence or stay; neither confirms nor denies; used with journalists, other guests, and callers regardless of how the enquiry is framed

**恐れ入りますが、ゲストの情報は一切お伝えできません。** *(Osoreirimasu ga, gesuto no jōhō wa issai o-tsutae dekimasen.)*
"I apologise, but I am unable to share any information about our guests.": a firmer version used when the initial refusal is pressed; maintains politeness while making the boundary clear

## Quiz

**A journalist calls the front desk asking to confirm whether a well-known celebrity is staying at the property. What is the correct response?**

- A. Confirm the stay but decline to provide the room number
- B. Deny that any celebrities are staying, even if one is
- C. Decline to share any information about guests: neither confirming nor denying
- D. Transfer the call to a manager to handle

**Correct Answer:** Decline to share any information about guests: neither confirming nor denying

**Explanation:** Confirming a guest is staying, even while withholding their room number, is still a breach of discretion, because it tells the caller exactly who is on the property. A flat denial is also wrong if it is untrue. The only correct response neither confirms nor denies, and it applies regardless of who is asking or how the question is framed.
