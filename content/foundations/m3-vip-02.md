title: Discretion — The Art of Silence
---

**Discretion** is the most essential quality in VIP service — and the hardest to train, because it is not a skill but a disposition. It is the understanding that what you know about a guest is held in trust, and that trust is absolute.

**What discretion means in practice:**

- **Never confirm or deny a VIP guest's presence** to anyone — media, other guests, callers, even people claiming to be family or colleagues. The standard response to any such enquiry is: *"I'm afraid I'm not able to share information about our guests."*
- **Never discuss guests with colleagues outside of operational necessity.** The detail that a famous guest ordered room service at 2 a.m. is not a story to share — not even in the staff room.
- **Never photograph, approach for personal reasons, or request autographs.** A VIP guest who is recognised by a staff member in a way that draws attention has been failed by that property.
- **Manage the environment**: If a VIP guest is dining, be alert to other guests who may recognise them. If necessary, manage sightlines, seat the VIP guest in a more private position, or gently redirect the attention of other guests.

**The test of discretion:**

True discretion is not simply avoiding obvious violations. It is behaving as though the guest's information does not exist for any purpose other than serving them. The test: if the guest were listening to every internal conversation about them, would they feel secure?

**Internal language:**

Use discreet codenames or room numbers rather than personal names in radio communications. *"Can housekeeping check room 812?"* is preferable to *"Can housekeeping go to the senator's suite?"*

## Quiz

**A journalist calls the front desk asking to confirm whether a well-known celebrity is staying at the property. What is the correct response?**

- A. Confirm the stay but decline to provide the room number
- B. Deny that any celebrities are staying, even if one is
- C. Decline to share any information about guests — neither confirming nor denying
- D. Transfer the call to a manager to handle

**Correct Answer:** Decline to share any information about guests — neither confirming nor denying

**Explanation:** Confirming a guest's presence — even without providing further details — is a breach of discretion. The standard response neither confirms nor denies any guest's stay, regardless of who is asking.
