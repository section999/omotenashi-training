title: Situational Judgment, Choosing the Right Keigo
---

Knowing keigo rules is one skill. Applying them instantly in the middle of a real service interaction is another. This lesson trains the judgment required to choose the correct keigo type in any situation, including edge cases that confuse even experienced staff.

## The Core Decision

Every keigo choice comes down to one question: who is performing the action?

<div class="keigo-card blue">
<strong>Step 1:</strong> Identify the subject of the action.<br>
Is it the guest? Is it the staff? Is it an object or a situation?<br><br>
<strong>Step 2:</strong> Choose the keigo type.<br>
Guest acting: 尊敬語<br>
Staff acting: 謙譲語<br>
Object, situation, or general information: 丁寧語<br><br>
<strong>Step 3:</strong> Apply the appropriate verb form and prefixes.<br>
Then build the full sentence.
</div>

## Practice Set 1: Who Is Acting?

<div class="keigo-card blue">
<strong>「お客様が食事をします」</strong><br>
Actor: guest. Type needed: 尊敬語.<br>
Correct: <span class="keigo-jp"><code>お客様がお食事を召し上がります。</code></span><br><br>
<strong>「私が荷物を運びます」</strong><br>
Actor: staff. Type needed: 謙譲語.<br>
Correct: <span class="keigo-jp"><code>お荷物をお運びいたします。</code></span><br><br>
<strong>「朝食は1階です」</strong><br>
Actor: none (fact). Type needed: 丁寧語.<br>
Correct: <span class="keigo-jp"><code>朝食は1階でございます。</code></span>
</div>

## Edge Case 1: Third Parties

When a third party (a colleague, a driver, another guest) is the subject, neither sonkeigo nor kenjogo is appropriate. Use standard polite language.

<div class="keigo-card blue">
<strong>Referring to a colleague to the guest:</strong><br>
<span class="keigo-badge bad">Wrong</span> <span class="keigo-jp"><code>担当者がいらっしゃいます。</code></span><br>
(Using sonkeigo for a colleague elevates them inappropriately)<br><br>
<span class="keigo-badge good">Correct</span> <span class="keigo-jp"><code>担当者がおります。</code></span><br>
(おります is the humble form, keeping the colleague below the guest)<br><br>
<strong>Referring to another hotel to the guest:</strong><br>
<span class="keigo-badge good">Correct</span> <span class="keigo-jp"><code>近隣のホテルをご案内することができます。</code></span><br>
(Neutral form; the guest is still the focus)
</div>

## Edge Case 2: The Guest's Family Members

When referring to the guest's family, use sonkeigo because they are guests too, and their relationship with the guest extends the honorific context.

<div class="keigo-card blue">
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>ご家族様もいらっしゃいますか。</code></span><br>
Will your family be joining you?<br><br>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>お子様はお召し上がりになりますか。</code></span><br>
Will your children be eating?<br><br>
Both ご家族様 and お子様 use honorific prefixes because they refer to people connected to the guest.
</div>

## Edge Case 3: When the Staff Member Is Also the Recipient

Sometimes staff receive something from a guest (a form, a payment, a request). The humble verb いただく applies even when the physical action is the guest handing something over.

<div class="keigo-card blue">
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>こちらの用紙にご記入いただけますでしょうか。</code></span><br>
Could you fill in this form?<br>
(Staff humbly receives the guest's completed form, いただく)<br><br>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>カードをお預かりいたします。</code></span><br>
I will take your card.<br>
(Staff humbly holds the card, お預かりいたします)<br><br>
The key: when the guest gives and the staff receives, the staff uses humble language because the act of receiving a guest's action is still a service act.
</div>

## Edge Case 4: Telephone Context

On the telephone, there is no visual confirmation of who is speaking. Keigo must be applied based on conversational role, not visual cues.

<div class="keigo-card blue">
When the caller says something: they are the subject. Apply sonkeigo to their action.<br>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>田中様がご予約なさっているとのことでございますね。</code></span><br>
I understand you have a reservation, Mr./Ms. Tanaka.<br><br>
When the staff member commits to an action: they are the subject. Apply kenjogo.<br>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>ただいまご確認いたします。少々お待ちくださいませ。</code></span><br>
I will confirm that now. Please wait a moment.
</div>

## Rapid Judgment Practice

<div class="keigo-card blue">
<strong>Situation A:</strong> The guest is walking to the elevator.<br>
Guest is acting. 尊敬語. <span class="keigo-jp"><code>エレベーターへいらっしゃいますか。こちらでございます。ご案内いたします。</code></span><br>
(いらっしゃいますか = 尊敬語 for the guest's movement; staff response follows in 丁寧語 + 謙譲語)<br><br>
<strong>Situation B:</strong> You are about to bring the guest's luggage to their room.<br>
Staff is acting. 謙譲語. <span class="keigo-jp"><code>お荷物をお部屋にお届けいたします。</code></span><br><br>
<strong>Situation C:</strong> You are telling the guest the pool is on the 3rd floor.<br>
No actor (fact). 丁寧語. <span class="keigo-jp"><code>プールは3階にございます。</code></span><br><br>
<strong>Situation D:</strong> The guest says they would like a wake-up call.<br>
Guest is acting. Receive it humbly. <span class="keigo-jp"><code>かしこまりました。ご指定の時間に承りました。</code></span>
</div>

## Key Phrases

<div class="keigo-card blue">
Guest acts: 尊敬語 → <span class="keigo-jp"><code>いらっしゃいます / 召し上がります / なさいます</code></span><br>
Staff acts: 謙譲語 → <span class="keigo-jp"><code>いたします / 参ります / 承ります</code></span><br>
Fact/situation: 丁寧語 → <span class="keigo-jp"><code>でございます / ございます</code></span><br>
Colleague (to guest): Humble → <span class="keigo-jp"><code>おります / 申し伝えます</code></span><br>
Guest's family: Sonkeigo → <span class="keigo-jp"><code>ご家族様 / お子様でいらっしゃいますか</code></span>
</div>

## Quiz

**A colleague will bring the guest's luggage to the room. How should the staff member explain this to the guest?**

- A. 「スタッフが荷物を持っていきます」
- B. 「スタッフがいらっしゃいます」
- C. 「スタッフがお荷物をお部屋にお届けいたします」
- D. 「スタッフが召し上がります」

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The staff member (not the guest) is performing the action, so 謙譲語 is required. お届けいたします uses the humble form of 届ける, which is correct for staff action. お荷物 correctly prefixes the Japanese-origin word 荷物 with お. Option A is plain language with no keigo. Option B uses いらっしゃいます (sonkeigo), which should not be applied to a colleague. Option D uses 召し上がります (sonkeigo for eating), which makes no sense in this context.
