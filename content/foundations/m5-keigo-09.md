title: Situational Judgment, Choosing the Right Keigo
---

Knowing keigo rules is one skill. Applying them instantly in the middle of a real service interaction is another. This lesson trains the judgment required to choose the correct keigo type in any situation, including edge cases that confuse even experienced staff.

## The Core Decision

Every keigo choice comes down to one question: who is performing the action?

| Subject | Keigo Type | Verb Approach |
|---|---|---|
| Guest is acting | <span>尊敬語</span> | Use an honorific verb form |
| Staff is acting | <span>謙譲語</span> | Use a humble verb form |
| Object, situation, or general information | <span>丁寧語</span> | Use a refined baseline form |

Once the subject is identified, apply the correct verb form and prefixes to build the full sentence.

## Edge Case 1: Third Parties

When a third party (a colleague, a driver, another guest) is the subject, neither sonkeigo nor kenjogo is appropriate. Use standard polite language.

<div class="keigo-pair">
<div class="bad-col">
<span class="keigo-pair-label">Wrong</span>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>担当者がいらっしゃいます。</code></span><br>
The person in charge is here.<br><br>
<span class="keigo-badge bad">Wrong</span> いらっしゃいます is 尊敬語; applying it to a colleague elevates them above the guest.
</div>
<div class="good-col">
<span class="keigo-pair-label">Correct</span>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>担当者がおります。</code></span><br>
The person in charge is here.<br><br>
<span class="keigo-badge good">Correct</span> おります is 謙譲語 for いる; it keeps the colleague in an appropriately lower position than the guest.
</div>
</div>

For a neutral third party such as another hotel, use a standard polite form:

<div class="keigo-card blue">
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>近隣のホテルをご案内することができます。</code></span><br>
<em>kinrin no hoteru o go-annai suru koto ga dekimasu.</em><br>
I can provide information about nearby hotels.
</div>

## Edge Case 2: The Guest's Family Members

When referring to the guest's family, use sonkeigo because they are guests too, and their relationship with the guest extends the honorific context.

<div class="keigo-card blue">
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>ご家族様もいらっしゃいますか。</code></span><br>
<em>go-kazoku-sama mo irasshaimasu ka.</em><br>
Will your family be joining you?<br><br>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>お子様はお召し上がりになりますか。</code></span><br>
<em>o-kosama wa o-meshiagari ni narimasu ka.</em><br>
Will your children be eating?
</div>

Both ご家族様 and お子様 use honorific prefixes because they refer to people connected to the guest, and the sonkeigo context extends to them.

## Edge Case 3: When the Staff Member Is Also the Recipient

Sometimes staff receive something from a guest (a form, a payment, a request). The humble verb いただく applies even when the physical action is the guest handing something over.

<div class="keigo-card blue">
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>こちらの用紙にご記入いただけますでしょうか。</code></span><br>
<em>kochira no youshi ni go-kinyu itadakemasu deshou ka.</em><br>
Could you fill in this form?<br><br>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>カードをお預かりいたします。</code></span><br>
<em>kaado o o-azukari itashimasu.</em><br>
I will take your card.
</div>

In the first phrase, the staff humbly receives the guest's completed form (いただく). In the second, the staff humbly holds the card (お預かりいたします). When the guest gives and the staff receives, humble language applies because receiving a guest's action is still a service act.

## Edge Case 4: Telephone Context

On the telephone, there is no visual confirmation of who is speaking. Keigo must be applied based on conversational role, not visual cues.

<div class="keigo-card blue">
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>田中様がご予約なさっているとのことでございますね。</code></span><br>
<em>tanaka-sama ga go-yoyaku nasatte iru to no koto de gozaimasu ne.</em><br>
I understand you have a reservation, Mr./Ms. Tanaka.<br><br>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>ただいまご確認いたします。少々お待ちくださいませ。</code></span><br>
<em>tadaima go-kakunin itashimasu. shoushou o-machi kudasaimase.</em><br>
I will confirm that now. Please wait a moment.
</div>

When the caller is the subject (performing an action), apply sonkeigo to that action. When the staff member commits to an action, apply kenjogo.

## Rapid Judgment Practice

<div class="keigo-card blue">
<strong>Situation A:</strong> The guest is walking to the elevator.<br>
Guest is acting → 尊敬語<br><br>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>エレベーターへいらっしゃいますか。こちらでございます。ご案内いたします。</code></span><br>
<em>erebeta e irasshaimasu ka. kochira de gozaimasu. go-annai itashimasu.</em><br>
Are you heading to the elevator? It is this way. I will guide you.<br><br>
<strong>Situation B:</strong> You are about to bring the guest's luggage to their room.<br>
Staff is acting → 謙譲語<br><br>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>お荷物をお部屋にお届けいたします。</code></span><br>
<em>o-nimotsu o o-heya ni o-todoke itashimasu.</em><br>
I will bring your luggage to your room.<br><br>
<strong>Situation C:</strong> You are telling the guest the pool is on the 3rd floor.<br>
No actor (fact) → 丁寧語<br><br>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>プールは3階にございます。</code></span><br>
<em>puru wa sankai ni gozaimasu.</em><br>
The pool is on the 3rd floor.<br><br>
<strong>Situation D:</strong> The guest requests a wake-up call.<br>
Staff receives the guest's request → 謙譲語<br><br>
<span class="keigo-jp"><code>かしこまりました。ご指定の時間に承りました。</code></span><br>
<em>kashikomarimashita. go-shitei no jikan ni uketamawarimashita.</em><br>
Certainly. I have noted your requested wake-up time.
</div>

## Quiz

**A colleague will bring the guest's luggage to the room. How should the staff member explain this to the guest?**

- A. スタッフが荷物を持っていきます
- B. スタッフがいらっしゃいます
- C. スタッフがお荷物をお部屋にお届けいたします
- D. スタッフが召し上がります

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The staff member (not the guest) is performing the action, so 謙譲語 is required. お届けいたします uses the humble form of 届ける, which is correct for staff action. お荷物 correctly prefixes the Japanese-origin word 荷物 with お. Option A is plain language with no keigo. Option B uses いらっしゃいます (sonkeigo), which should not be applied to a colleague. Option D uses 召し上がります (sonkeigo for eating), which makes no sense in this context.
