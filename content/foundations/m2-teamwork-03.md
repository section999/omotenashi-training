title: The Invisible Handover — Passing Guests Between Departments
---

One of the defining qualities of world-class hospitality is that the guest never feels transferred — they feel continuously welcomed. Yet in practice, a guest's stay involves many different departments: reception, bellservice, restaurant, housekeeping, concierge, spa. Each time responsibility shifts, there is a risk of the guest feeling passed on rather than accompanied.

**The visible vs. invisible handover:**

A **visible handover** is when the guest is aware they are being transferred: "I'll just hand you over to my colleague." This is sometimes necessary, but it signals a boundary in the team's care.

An **invisible handover** is when the receiving colleague is already informed, already ready, and greets the guest as if they have been expecting them all along. The guest experiences continuity.

**How to execute an invisible handover:**

1. **Communicate ahead** (renraku): Before the guest arrives at the next touchpoint, relay the relevant information — name, room number, any preferences or special needs, the context of the interaction so far.
2. **Introduce personally** where possible: Walk the guest to the next colleague rather than directing them. Introduce the colleague by name: *"こちらは田中がご案内いたします"* — "My colleague Tanaka will assist you from here."
3. **Brief the receiving colleague**: Not just the facts, but the mood — is the guest relaxed or in a hurry? Are they pleased or mildly frustrated? This emotional context shapes how the receiving colleague opens the interaction.
4. **Follow up**: If you were involved in a guest issue or special request, check later whether it was resolved. Close the loop yourself.

**The guest's experience:**
A guest who is seamlessly passed between departments feels that the property operates as one entity with a single focus: their comfort. This is the team version of omotenashi.

## Key Phrases

**引き継ぎ (hikitsugi)** — handover; the act of transferring responsibility and information from one staff member to another between departments or shifts

**こちらは田中がご案内いたします。** *(Kochira wa Tanaka ga go-annai itashimasu.)*
"My colleague Tanaka will assist you from here." — the preferred phrase for a personal introduction during a warm, invisible handover

**事前にご連絡いたします。** *(Jizen ni go-renraku itashimasu.)*
"I will contact them in advance." — the commitment that makes an invisible handover possible; renraku before the guest arrives at the next touchpoint

## Quiz

**What distinguishes an invisible handover from a visible one?**

- A. An invisible handover happens without the guest's knowledge at all
- B. The receiving colleague is already informed and prepared, so the guest experiences continuity rather than a transfer
- C. An invisible handover occurs only between departments on the same floor
- D. It refers to handovers that happen during shift changes, not guest-to-department transfers

**Correct Answer:** The receiving colleague is already informed and prepared, so the guest experiences continuity rather than a transfer

**Explanation:** An invisible handover relies on proactive communication (renraku) so that the next colleague can receive the guest as if they already know them. The guest never feels passed on — they feel accompanied.
